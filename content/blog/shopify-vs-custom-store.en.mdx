# Shopify vs a Custom Store: The Real Three-Year Calculation

Most comparisons on this subject are written by someone selling one of the two options, so they arrive at a predetermined conclusion. This guide compares with numbers across three years — and ends with a conclusion a development agency might not enjoy.

**The short answer: a hosted platform is genuinely cheaper up to a certain sales volume. The tipping point is something you should calculate, not guess.**

---

## The two options in one sentence each

**Hosted platform ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/), [Salla](https://salla.sa/), [Zid](https://zid.sa/)):** you rent a pre-built store. You start selling within days at low upfront cost, in exchange for a permanent monthly subscription and sometimes a percentage of sales.

**Custom store:** you own the platform entirely. Much higher upfront cost, no platform subscription and no sales commission, but you carry responsibility for maintenance, hosting, and development.

---

## The three-year calculation

This is the heart of the decision. The figures below assume a mid-size store with $50,000 in annual sales:

| Line item | Hosted platform | Custom store |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | $5,000 | $25,000 |
| Platform subscription (3 years) | $5,400 | $0 |
| Hosting and maintenance (3 years) | Included | $7,500 |
| Platform commission on sales* | $4,500 | $0 |
| Paid apps and extensions | $2,700 | $1,500 |
| **Three-year total** | **$17,600** | **$34,000** |

*\* Assuming a 3% platform commission. Rates vary by plan and payment gateway.*

**The verdict at this volume: the hosted platform is roughly $16,400 cheaper.** That is a genuine result, not a courtesy.

---

## The tipping point: when does the maths flip?

The decisive variable is **the platform's commission on your sales** — because it is the only line item that grows with your success while everything else stays roughly fixed.

| Annual sales | Platform commission (3%) over 3 years | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| $50,000 | $4,500 | Hosted clearly cheaper |
| $150,000 | $13,500 | Approximate break-even |
| $500,000 | $45,000 | Custom clearly cheaper |
| $1,000,000 | $90,000 | Custom cheaper by a wide margin |

**The practical rule:** once your sales pass roughly **$150,000 annually**, platform commission alone exceeds the cost of building a store you own. Below that figure, a hosted platform is a commercially correct decision — not a compromise.

> **Calculate your own number:** `annual platform commission = projected sales × applicable rate`. Compare it against build cost spread over three years plus annual maintenance. If the commission is higher, building earns its cost.

## What the table doesn't show

Numbers alone don't settle the decision. These are real factors with no direct figure:

**In favour of a hosted platform:**
- **Speed** — you sell within days rather than months. In a competitive market, a three-month delay is a real cost.
- **Reliability** — the infrastructure is tested across thousands of stores; yours won't collapse in peak season.
- **Automatic security updates** — you don't need a team watching them.
- **Built-in local compliance** — Salla and Zid support Fatoora (ZATCA), Mada, Tabby, and Tamara out of the box.

**In favour of a custom store:**
- **Ownership** — nobody can raise prices or change terms on you.
- **Performance** — an optimised store is faster, which drives both conversion and search ranking.
- **Flexibility** — an unconventional sales model (complex subscriptions, negotiated pricing, wholesale) may be unsupported by platforms.
- **Deep integration** — connecting to an existing ERP or accounting system is far easier.

## An overlooked third option: WooCommerce

Between the two extremes sits a middle path: **an open-source foundation you build on**.

- **Upfront cost:** $15,000 – $40,000 — higher than hosted, lower than fully custom.
- **No platform subscription and no sales commission.**
- **Wide flexibility** through thousands of extensions.
- **The drawback:** it needs regular technical maintenance and security updates, and slows down as plugins accumulate.

It suits anyone who has outgrown a hosted platform but doesn't need a full build from scratch.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should I move from a hosted platform to a custom store?**

The clearest indicator is numerical: once annual sales pass roughly $150,000, platform commission alone exceeds the cost of building a store you own. The second indicator is functional: finding yourself working around platform limitations to execute a basic sales operation in your business model.

**Do I lose my data and customers when migrating?**

Not if you plan for it. Products, customers, and orders are exportable from most platforms. What is hard to move is **links and search ranking** — so plan 301 redirects from every old URL to its new counterpart, or you lose the ranking you earned.

**Is a custom store genuinely faster?**

Usually yes, because it carries only what you need without generic layers. But the difference depends on execution quality — a poorly built custom store can be slower than an optimised hosted platform. Ask for a commitment to measurable Core Web Vitals standards rather than promises.

**What does running a custom store cost annually?**

Between $2,000 and $6,000 per year for hosting, maintenance, and security updates, plus payment gateway commission (2.5–3% per transaction). Add the cost of any new development you request later — a custom store doesn't evolve automatically the way platforms do.

**Can I start hosted and build later?**

Yes, and this is the most rational path for most projects. You test the market with minimal risk, build a customer base and real sales data, then build your custom store with clear requirements grounded in actual experience rather than assumptions.

**What is the biggest mistake store owners make?**

Building an expensive custom store before verifying the product sells at all. Build cost isn't the largest loss in that case — it's the months that could have been spent actually testing the market.

## Conclusion

**Under $150,000 in annual sales:** a hosted platform is clearly cheaper — a commercially correct decision, not a compromise.

**Above that:** start by calculating annual platform commission. If it exceeds build cost spread over three years, a custom store earns its place.

**In every case:** don't build a custom store before proving your product sells. The smarter path is usually to start hosted and migrate when your numbers demand it.
