# How Much Does a Clinic Appointment Booking App Cost? (2026 Guide)

**A booking and reminder app for a single clinic costs $5,000–$15,000 (SAR 19,000–56,500 / AED 18,500–55,000 / QAR 18,000–54,500) and takes 6–10 weeks. Add online payment, several doctors or branches, or a link to your clinic management system, and it becomes a mid-complexity app at $15,000–$45,000 (SAR 56,500–169,000 / AED 55,000–165,500 / QAR 54,500–164,000).** Video consultations and medical records turn it into an advanced platform at $45,000–$100,000.

Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. The rest of this guide explains which tier your clinic is in, which add-ons change the number, and the Saudi rules that shape the build. ## What moves a clinic up a tier

**The number of roles.** An app for patients alone is a simple app. Add doctors, receptionists and management, each with their own screens and permissions, and you are in the mid tier.

**Integration with your current system.** If you already run a clinic management system, connecting the app to it is usually the hardest part of the project, especially if that system is old or has no API. Integration alone is a common reason a clinic lands in the mid tier rather than the simple one.

**Medical records.** Showing appointments is one thing; storing diagnoses and prescriptions is another. It raises the security, compliance and testing work together, and it is what typically pushes a project into the advanced tier.

**Remote consultations.** Video calling turns a booking app into a telehealth service, with the licensing that comes with it.

## Regulatory requirements in Saudi Arabia

**Health data is sensitive data.** Article 1 of the [Personal Data Protection Law](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/08/PersonalDataProtectionLaw.pdf) lists health data among the categories of sensitive data, and defines it as any personal data about a person's physical, mental or psychological condition or the health services they received. Article 23 requires extra controls on health data, including limiting access to medical files to the minimum number of staff needed to provide care. Article 20 requires the controller to notify the regulator of any breach, and to notify patients where the breach could harm them. Plan consent screens, access roles and audit logs into the first version; retrofitting them is the expensive path.

**Booking alone is not a medical device.** The SFDA's [Guidance on Software as a Medical Device](https://sfda.gov.sa/en/guide/guidance-software-medical-device) states that software enabling clinical communication and workflow, including patient registration and scheduling visits, is not software as a medical device. That changes once the app supports diagnosis, clinical decisions or monitoring a condition: whether it is a medical device then depends on its intended use, and an AI component in that role deserves specialist regulatory advice before you build it.

**Telehealth is a separate step.** Video consultations bring healthcare-licensing questions for the clinic itself, not only for the software. Treat them as a phase two, decided with legal advice.

> **Practical recommendation:** if your goal is booking and reminders, keep clinical features out of the first version. You stay clear of medical-device questions, launch in the simple or mid tier, and carry far less compliance work. Targeting the UAE instead? ## Is the app worth it? Calculate it with your own numbers

How big is the no-show problem? A study of 1,087,979 outpatient appointments at King Faisal Specialist Hospital in Riyadh, published in [Annals of Saudi Medicine in 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6894458/), recorded a no-show rate of 11.3% for 2014. Your clinic's rate could be higher or lower, so measure it before deciding.

**Monthly loss from no-shows = monthly appointments × no-show rate × average appointment value**

An illustrative example, not a forecast: 600 appointments a month at an 11.3% no-show rate and an average value of 200 SAR:

`600 × 0.113 × 200 = 13,560 SAR lost each month`

Suppose reminders and one-tap rescheduling bring the rate down to 8%. The loss falls to 9,600 SAR, a saving of 3,960 SAR a month. Against a simple-tier build of SAR 19,000–56,500, that pays back in roughly 5 to 14 months, before counting the receptionist time it frees. Put your own figures in; if you do not measure your no-show rate today, measure it for one month first.

Remember the running costs too: annual maintenance is 15–20% of the build cost, and publishing on the App Store needs an [Apple Developer Program membership at 99 USD a year](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/).

## A cheaper path: do you need an app at all?

- **A booking page on your website** is priced as part of a mid-size company website, $8,000–$25,000 (SAR 30,000–94,000 / AED 29,500–92,000 / QAR 29,000–91,000), and patients book from a browser without downloading anything.
- **Off-the-shelf booking software** on a monthly subscription suits a single small clinic that does not need its own brand in the app stores.
- **A custom app** earns its cost when you need your own identity, deep integration with your clinic system, many repeat patients, or several branches.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a booking-and-reminders app for one clinic cost?

It is a simple app on our rate card: $5,000–$15,000 (SAR 19,000–56,500 / AED 18,500–55,000 / QAR 18,000–54,500), built in 6–10 weeks, covering booking, cancelling, rescheduling, reminders and a basic patient profile.

### Does a booking-only clinic app need SFDA registration?

Not on the basis of booking alone. The SFDA's software guidance says software for patient registration and scheduling visits is not software as a medical device. If you later add diagnosis support, clinical decision support or condition monitoring, check its classification before building that feature.

### Which add-on pays back first for a clinic?

Push notifications for reminders, at $1,500–$3,000 (SAR 5,600–11,500 / AED 5,500–11,000 / QAR 5,500–11,000). They act on no-shows directly and carry no per-message cost, unlike SMS.

### What does adding video consultations do to the budget?

It moves the project into the advanced tier, $45,000–$100,000 (SAR 169,000–375,000 / AED 165,500–367,500 / QAR 164,000–364,000), because video usually arrives with medical records, prescriptions and stricter access controls, and it raises telehealth licensing questions for the clinic.

### How does Saudi PDPL treat patient data differently from other customer data?

Health data is listed as sensitive data in Article 1, and Article 23 adds extra controls such as limiting access to medical files to the staff who need it. Design consent, access roles and breach notification into the first release.

### Can a booking page on our website replace the app?

For a small clinic, often yes. A booking page is priced within a mid-size company website at $8,000–$25,000 and needs no download. An app is worth it when you need reliable reminders, serve many repeat patients, or run several branches.
