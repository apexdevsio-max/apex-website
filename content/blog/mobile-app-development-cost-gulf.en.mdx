# How Much Does It Cost to Build a Mobile App in the Gulf? (2026 Guide)

**A mid-complexity app costs SAR 56,500–169,000 in Saudi Arabia, AED 55,000–165,500 in the UAE and QAR 54,500–164,000 in Qatar.** That is the same $15,000–$45,000 tier as anywhere else: the riyal, dirham and Qatari riyal are pegged to the dollar, so the conversion is fixed. What the Gulf adds is local payment rails, Arabic right-to-left interfaces and local data protection law.

Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. This guide covers only what differs in the Gulf. For the general pricing factors, feature add-ons, maintenance and three-year cost, read the pillar guide, [mobile app development cost in 2026](/en/blog/mobile-app-development-cost).

## Local payment rails: the main regional cost

Card payments through one gateway, with Apple Pay and Google Pay, are part of a mid-tier app, or the in-app payments add-on ($3,000–$7,000) on a simple app. **Every additional local gateway costs $1,500–$5,000** (SAR 5,600–19,000 / AED 5,500–18,500 / QAR 5,500–18,000), because each has its own API, sandbox and certification. Which rails matter depends on the country:

**Saudi Arabia: mada.** [mada is a national payment system owned by the Saudi Central Bank](https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-US/payment/Pages/mada.aspx), and mada cards can pay at e-commerce merchants with 3-D Secure verification. A Saudi consumer app should accept it. If your gateway already accepts mada alongside Visa and Mastercard, mada needs no separate integration. Saudi wallets with their own API, such as STC Pay (whose operator has [become STC Bank, licensed by SAMA as a digital bank](https://stcbank.com.sa/en/notice-of-transformation-to-stc-bank)), count as an additional gateway.

**UAE: Jaywan.** Jaywan is [the UAE's first domestic card scheme](https://centralbank.ae/media/2crjrusv/jaywan-the-uae-s-first-domestic-card-scheme-en.pdf), run by Al Etihad Payments, a subsidiary of the Central Bank of the UAE. It comes as debit, prepaid and credit cards and is designed for online transactions as well as ATMs and point-of-sale terminals. Banks issue both Jaywan-only (mono-badge) cards and cards co-badged with international networks, so ask your gateway whether it accepts Jaywan-only cards before you assume coverage.

**Qatar: Fawran.** The Qatar Central Bank [launched Fawran in March 2024](https://www.qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2024-03/07/0036-qcb-launches-'fawran'-instant-payment-service). It is an instant bank-transfer service that works around the clock, lets people pay using a mobile number or alias instead of an IBAN, and caps each transfer at QR 50,000. It matters if you invoice customers or collect larger payments by transfer rather than by card.

## Arabic and right-to-left

Arabic is not a translation pass at the end. The whole layout mirrors, numbers and dates need locale rules, and mixed Arabic-English text has to render correctly. When Arabic and English are both in the brief from the start, they are designed and built together inside the tier price. Retrofitting right-to-left into a finished left-to-right design means redoing screens that were already approved, and that rework is scoped and priced separately.

[Tafawwoq](/en/portfolio/tafawwoq-educational-app), the Arabic education app we built in Flutter, was designed right-to-left from the first screen. For the engineering side see [Flutter RTL for Arabic](/en/blog/flutter-rtl-arabic-guide) and the [Arabic localisation guide](/en/blog/arabic-localization-guide).

## Data protection and sector rules

Compliance in the Gulf is less about extra features and more about decisions that shape the architecture, chiefly where data is stored and who can access it. Make these decisions before the build; changing hosting after launch is expensive.

- **Saudi Arabia.** The [Personal Data Protection Law](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/08/PersonalDataProtectionLaw.pdf) applies to processing in the Kingdom and to processing of personal data of people residing in the Kingdom by any party outside it (Article 2). Health data is classed as sensitive data (Article 1), and transfers outside the Kingdom must meet the conditions in Article 29. See the [PDPL compliance guide](/en/blog/pdpl-compliance-guide).
- **UAE.** Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 governs personal data processing, while the DIFC has its own Data Protection Law, as the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws) lists. See the [UAE data protection guide](/en/blog/uae-data-protection-guide).
- **Sector regulators.** Fintech in Saudi Arabia falls under SAMA ([fintech and SAMA](/en/blog/fintech-sama-compliance)); health apps in the UAE have their own requirements ([health apps in the UAE](/en/blog/health-apps-uae-compliance)).

For where your servers can sit in each country, see [data residency in the Gulf](/en/blog/data-residency-gulf).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an app built for Saudi Arabia cost more than the same app elsewhere?

No. The tiers are the same, and the riyal is pegged at 3.75 to the dollar, so a mid-complexity app is SAR 56,500–169,000 as of November 2026. The extra cost comes from additional local gateways at $1,500–$5,000 each, not from the market.

### Do I need a separate integration to accept mada cards?

Not if your payment gateway already accepts mada alongside Visa and Mastercard; then mada is part of the first gateway. A wallet or provider with its own API is an additional gateway at $1,500–$5,000 (SAR 5,600–19,000).

### Which local payment methods should a UAE or Qatar app plan for?

In the UAE, check that your gateway accepts Jaywan, the domestic card scheme of the Central Bank of the UAE's payments subsidiary. In Qatar, Fawran instant transfers, capped at QR 50,000 per transaction, matter if you collect payments by bank transfer.

### Is Arabic support charged as an extra?

Not when Arabic and English are both in the brief from day one; they are designed and built together within the tier. Retrofitting right-to-left into a finished English-only design is rework that is scoped separately.

### Does the Saudi PDPL apply if my servers are outside the Kingdom?

Yes. Article 2 extends the law to processing of personal data of people residing in the Kingdom by any party outside it, and Article 29 sets the conditions for transfers abroad. Choose your hosting with that in mind.
