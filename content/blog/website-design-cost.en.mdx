# Professional Website Design Cost in 2026

A professional website costs **$1,500 to $5,000** for a landing page or small brochure site, **$8,000 to $25,000** for a mid-size company site, and **$25,000 to $80,000** for a custom web application, as of November 2026. An online store is priced separately, at $20,000 to $65,000.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Mid-size company website | $8,000 – $25,000 | 30,000 – 94,000 | 29,500 – 92,000 | 29,000 – 91,000 | 6 – 12 weeks |

## What moves the price within a tier

**Unique page designs, not page count.** A ten-page site built from three page layouts costs less than a five-page site where every page is designed from scratch. Count the distinct layouts in a quote, not the pages.

**Template or custom design.** A ready-made theme is the fastest way to the bottom of a tier, at the cost of looking like other sites using it. Custom design gives you a visual identity and moves you up the range. [Template or custom design?](/en/blog/template-vs-custom-design) covers the trade-off.

**Editing the content yourself.** Connecting the site to a content management system costs more upfront and saves you a developer fee for every future text change. For a site whose content changes monthly, it pays for itself.

**Arabic and English together.** A bilingual site is not two sites, because structure and features are shared. It does mean mirrored right-to-left layouts, Arabic web fonts, translated content and testing in both directions. Planned from the first wireframe it fits inside the tier; retrofitted onto an English-only design, it costs noticeably more. [Arabic web fonts](/en/blog/arabic-web-fonts) explains the performance side.

**Functions that change the tier.** A contact form is part of any tier. Accounts, online payments, booking with scheduling rules or an interactive price calculator turn a company site into a custom web application, which is the $25,000–$80,000 tier, not an add-on to the company-site price.

## What a live website costs each year

A quote covers the build. A live site has running costs, and leaving them out means a surprise after year one.

| Item | What to budget | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance and security updates | 15 – 20% of the build cost per year | Core, plugin and dependency updates, fixes, small changes |
| Domain name | The registrar's published price | Must be registered in your company's name |
| Hosting | The host's published plan price | Depends on traffic and whether the site is static or database-driven |
| SSL certificate | Can be free | [Let's Encrypt](https://letsencrypt.org/about/) issues trusted certificates "at zero cost" |
| Backups | Usually part of the hosting plan | Confirm frequency and that a restore has been tested |

On a $12,000 company site, maintenance at 15–20% is $1,800–$2,400 a year. Hosting and domain come on top and are priced by the provider, so get those figures from their pricing pages before you sign. A quote that never mentions any of this is incomplete.

## Costs quotes leave out

- **Content writing.** Most quotes assume you will supply the text. If you will not, ask for writing to be priced as a separate line.
- **Photography.** Your team, premises and products. Stock photos weaken trust because visitors recognise them.
- **Translation.** A bilingual site needs properly translated copy; machine translation shows.
- **Basic search optimisation.** Page titles, descriptions, structured headings and a sitemap. Some quotes exclude it entirely.
- **Admin training.** An hour or two of training saves you edit fees for years.
- **Migrating an old site.** Moving content and redirecting old URLs takes time and protects your rankings; it deserves its own line. [Redesigning a website](/en/blog/website-redesign-guide) covers how.

> **The one question to ask:** "What does this quote **not** include?" It reveals more than any written line item.

## How to read a quote

A professional quote states these explicitly. If one is missing, ask before signing:

1. **Pages and unique layouts**, named rather than described.
2. **Revision rounds included**, and the price of extra rounds.
3. **Who supplies content and images**: you or the agency.
4. **Mobile behaviour**, stated rather than assumed.
5. **A measurable speed target.** Google's [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) define "good" as Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint of 200 milliseconds or less, and Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less. Ask for those in writing.
6. **Ownership of code, domain and hosting**, all in your name.
7. **Warranty period after delivery**, and exactly what it covers.
8. **A phased timeline**, not one delivery date at the end.

## WordPress or a custom build?

**WordPress** suits brochure sites and blogs: quicker to build, cheaper, and easy to edit yourself. The cost is ongoing upkeep: WordPress's own [hardening guidance](https://developer.wordpress.org/advanced-administration/security/hardening/) says to keep up to date with the latest version and to make sure plugins are always updated, and sites slow down as plugins accumulate.

**A custom build** (for example with Next.js) suits sites where speed and search ranking matter commercially, or that need functions a theme cannot do. Higher upfront cost, fewer moving parts to patch afterwards.

**Rule of thumb:** an informational site whose content changes often is a WordPress job. A site that is part of your product, or your main source of customers, earns the custom build. [WordPress or Next.js?](/en/blog/wordpress-vs-nextjs) goes deeper.

## Where the schedule actually goes

| Site type | Timeline | What dominates it |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page | 1 – 2 weeks | Copy and design approval |
| Small brochure site | 3 – 5 weeks | Content, not code |
| Company site with blog | 6 – 12 weeks | Content and CMS setup |
| Online store | 4 – 7 months | Products, payments, shipping rules |
| Custom web application | 4 – 8 months | Specification and integrations |

**The delay nobody plans for is content.** Design and development can be ready while the site waits weeks for copy, product photos and team bios. Start writing on the day the project starts, not after the design is approved.

## Who owns what when the project ends

Settle this in the contract. Before the final payment, confirm in writing that you own:

- **The domain name**, registered to your company, not the agency's account. A domain in a vendor's name gives them leverage over your whole online presence.
- **The hosting account**, or at minimum full administrative access.
- **The source code** and the design files.
- **Every third-party account**: analytics, email, CDN, payment gateway.

Ask: "If we part ways next month, what do we walk away with, and what stops working?" A related trap is a proprietary CMS that only one agency can maintain; prefer a standard platform or a documented export path. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much is a small business website in riyals or dirhams?

A landing page or small brochure site is SAR 5,600–19,000, AED 5,500–18,500 or QAR 5,500–18,000 ($1,500–$5,000) as of November 2026. A larger company site with more layouts and a CMS is SAR 30,000–94,000 ($8,000–$25,000).

### Does an Arabic and English site move me into a higher tier?

Not by itself. When both languages are planned from the first wireframe, a bilingual company site stays in the $8,000–$25,000 tier. What pushes the cost up is adding Arabic later to a layout designed only for left-to-right text.

### Which tier is a website with online booking or payments?

A site with an enquiry form stays in its tier. Once visitors log in, pay online or book against real availability, it is a custom web application: $25,000–$80,000 (SAR 94,000–300,000).

### What will my website cost each year after launch?

Maintenance at 15–20% of the build cost — $1,800–$2,400 on a $12,000 site — plus the domain and hosting at your providers' published prices. The SSL certificate can be free through Let's Encrypt.

### Who should register the domain, me or the agency?

You. Register it in your company's name from day one and give the agency access, not ownership. A domain held in a vendor's account is the most common lock-in in web projects.

### What speed should I hold the agency to?

The Core Web Vitals "good" thresholds: Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint of 200 milliseconds or less, and Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less. Put them in the contract as acceptance criteria.
