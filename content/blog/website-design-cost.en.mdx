# Professional Website Design Cost in 2026

Asking "how much does a website cost?" is like asking "how much does a car cost?" — the answer depends entirely on what you actually need. But that does not mean the number is unknowable: the market has clear ranges, and understanding them stops you from overpaying or accepting a cheap quote you will pay for later.

**The short answer: a professional brochure site costs between $1,500 and $8,000. A mid-size company site runs $8,000 to $25,000. A custom web platform exceeds $50,000.**

This guide explains what places your project within those ranges, the ongoing costs no quote mentions, and how to read an offer before signing.

---

## What determines website cost?

Five factors drive most of the difference between one quote and another.

**Page count and uniqueness.** A 5-page site using one consistent template is far cheaper than a 5-page site where each page has its own design. What costs money is not the number of pages but the number of **unique designs**.

**Design: template or custom?** A ready-made template cuts cost by 50–70%, but gives you an appearance shared with thousands of other sites. Custom design builds a distinct visual identity and costs between $2,000 and $15,000 depending on complexity.

**Content management system.** Do you want to edit text yourself? Wiring the site to an admin panel adds $1,000–$4,000, but saves you a fee for every future edit.

**Special functionality.** A simple contact form is one thing; an appointment booking system, an interactive price calculator, or a members area is something else entirely. Each function is priced separately.

**Arabic and English together.** A bilingual site is not double the cost, but it adds 25–40%: mirrored layouts for RTL support, appropriate Arabic typefaces, professionally translated content, and double the testing.

## The annual costs everyone forgets

A quote covers the build. A live website has ongoing costs, and ignoring them means a surprise after year one:

| Item | Annual cost | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | $15 – $60 | A `.sa` or `.qa` domain may require documentation |
| Hosting | $100 – $1,200 | Depends on traffic and site type |
| SSL certificate | $0 – $200 | Usually free via Let's Encrypt |
| Maintenance and security updates | $500 – $3,000 | Practically mandatory, not optional |
| Backups | $50 – $300 | Far cheaper than losing the site |

**Realistic total: $700 – $4,500 per year** depending on site size. Anyone quoting you a website without mentioning these items has given you an incomplete offer.

## Hidden costs in quotes

These are real items that surprise site owners after signing:

- **Content writing** — most quotes assume you will supply the text. Professional copywriting costs $50–$200 per page, translation $30–$120.
- **Photography** — pictures of your team, premises, and products. Stock images weaken credibility because visitors recognise them.
- **Basic search optimisation** — some quotes exclude it entirely. A site without SEO is a site nobody finds.
- **Admin panel training** — two hours of training saves you edit fees for years.
- **Migrating the old site** — if you have an existing site, moving content and links takes time and deserves pricing.

> **Practical rule:** ask directly, "what does this quote **not** include?" That question reveals more than any written line item.

## How to read a quote

A professional quote states these explicitly. The absence of any one is a question worth asking before signing:

1. **Page count and unique designs** — named, not described generally.
2. **Number of free revision rounds** — and the cost beyond them.
3. **Who supplies content and images** — you or them?
4. **Mobile responsiveness** — should be a given, but confirm it is stated.
5. **Site speed** — ask for a commitment to a measurable standard such as [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals).
6. **Ownership of code, domain, and hosting** — all should be in your name.
7. **Warranty period after delivery** — and exactly what it covers.
8. **A phased timeline** — not a single delivery date at the end.

**The riskiest item: domain ownership.** Some providers register the domain in their own name, making a later provider change practically impossible. Confirm the domain is registered **in your name or your company's** from day one.

## WordPress or a custom build?

**WordPress** suits brochure sites and blogs: faster to build, cheaper, and you can edit content yourself. Its drawbacks are the need for regular security updates and slowdown as plugins accumulate.

**A custom build** (using modern technology such as Next.js) suits sites where performance, speed, and search ranking matter, or that need unconventional functionality. Higher upfront cost, lower long-term maintenance, and noticeably faster.

**The practical rule:** if your site is informational and its content changes often, WordPress is the right call. ## What a website timeline actually looks like

Cost and timeline move together, and unrealistic schedule expectations are a frequent source of friction.

| Site type | Timeline | What dominates the schedule |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page | 1 – 2 weeks | Copy and design approval |
| Brochure site (5–8 pages) | 3 – 5 weeks | Content, not build |
| Company site with blog | 5 – 8 weeks | Content and CMS setup |
| Online store | 8 – 14 weeks | Products, payments, shipping rules |
| Custom platform | 3 – 6 months | Specification and integrations |

**The delay nobody plans for is content.** Design and development can be ready while the site waits weeks on copy, product photos, and team bios. If you take one practical step from this guide: start writing content the day the project starts, not after the design is approved.

## Who owns what when the project ends

This is settled in the contract or it is settled badly. Before the final payment clears, confirm in writing that you own:

- **The domain name** — registered to your company, not the agency's account. This is the single most common trap: a domain in a vendor's name gives them leverage over your entire online presence.
- **The hosting account** — or at minimum, administrative access.
- **The source code** and the design files.
- **All third-party accounts** — analytics, email, CDN, payment gateway.

**Ask specifically:** "If we part ways next month, what do we walk away with, and what stops working?" The answer tells you more than any portfolio.

A related trap is the proprietary CMS — a custom admin system only that agency can maintain. It appears convenient and functions as lock-in: your content is not portable, and you cannot get competitive quotes for future work. Prefer a standard platform, or ensure a documented export path exists.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a small business website cost?

A professional 5 to 8 page brochure site ranges from $1,500 to $8,000. Variation within that range comes from the number of unique designs, whether it is monolingual or bilingual, and whether it includes an admin panel for editing content.

### How long does website design take?

Landing page: 1–2 weeks. Brochure site: 2–5 weeks. Mid-size company site: 6–12 weeks. Custom platform: 4–8 months. Delays in the client supplying content and images are the most common cause of overrun — not design or development.

### Do I pay once or subscribe monthly?

The build is normally a one-time cost paid in instalments tied to milestones. But ongoing annual costs for domain, hosting, and maintenance remain, between $700 and $4,500. Be wary of offers that tie you to a monthly subscription without owning the site.

### What is the difference between a brochure site and an online store?

A brochure site presents your services and collects enquiries. A store handles products, inventory, payments, and shipping — a more complex and more expensive product. See our [Saudi e-commerce cost guide](/en/blog/ecommerce-development-cost-saudi) if you intend to sell online.

### Does a bilingual site double the cost?

No. It typically adds only 25–40%, because structure and functionality are shared. The increase covers mirrored RTL layouts, Arabic typefaces, professional translation, and double testing. In the Gulf market, a monolingual site loses a large share of its audience.

### Do I need a website if I have social media accounts?

Yes. Your accounts are rented, not owned — an algorithm change or account suspension erases your reach entirely. A website is an asset you own, appears in search results, and gives your business credibility that an account alone does not.
