# Fintech in Saudi Arabia: The Central Bank Framework for Developers

If your product holds customers' money, moves it for merchants, initiates payments, reads bank account data or offers Buy Now Pay Later, it needs authorisation from the **Saudi Central Bank (SAMA)** before launch. If it deals in securities instead, such as equity crowdfunding or robo-advice, the **Capital Market Authority (CMA)** is your regulator, through its FinTech Lab. The timelines are published: SAMA aims to decide a complete payment licence application within **90 calendar days**, and its Regulatory Sandbox runs live tests for **6 to 12 months**.

This guide maps those routes and explains what each means for the technical build.

> Written from an engineering perspective, not legal advice. Current as of November 2026.

SAMA authorisation sits on top of data protection law, not in place of it. For the obligations that apply in every sector, see the [Gulf regulatory compliance guide](/en/blog/gulf-compliance-guide). Confirm your regulatory status with a specialist advisor and with the regulator before you commit budget.

## First: which regulator, which route?

| What your product does | Regulator | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Shows general financial information, or budgets from manual entry | Usually none | Confirm with an advisor that no activity below applies |
| Holds stored value (e-wallet) | SAMA | Electronic Money Institution licence (Micro or Major) |
| Accepts or moves payments for others without issuing e-money | SAMA | Payment Institution licence (Micro or Major) |
| Reads bank account data or initiates payments from a bank account | SAMA | Open banking licensing |
| Buy Now Pay Later | SAMA | BNPL company licence |
| A new model SAMA's current rules do not cover | SAMA | Regulatory Sandbox |
| Securities activity (equity crowdfunding, robo-advice) | CMA | FinTech Experimental Permit through the FinTech Lab |

**The practical rule:** if your product touches other people's money or banking data, assume it is regulated until an advisor confirms otherwise.

The licence decides the architecture: what you may store, which providers you must use, and which controls are audited. That is why the regulatory track should start alongside design, and why our [mobile app team](/en/services/mobile-apps) asks the licensing question before quoting a fintech build.

## Payment services: the four SAMA licences

SAMA's [Guidelines to Apply for Payment Service Providers License](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/node/10951) set out four licence types:

- **Micro Electronic Money Institution** (e-wallet)
- **Major Electronic Money Institution** (e-wallet)
- **Micro Payment Institution**
- **Major Payment Institution**

The process has two stages. **In-principle approval** comes first and lets you complete pre-licensing steps such as commercial registration with the Ministry of Commerce. The **licensing stage** follows, and you can start the activity only after it. SAMA states it will notify its decision in writing **within 90 calendar days** of telling you the application is complete, and will give a revised estimate if it needs longer.

If you do not want to hold a licence, the common alternative is to integrate with a SAMA-licensed payment provider that handles the regulated side while you build the product layer. Verifying that the provider is licensed is your responsibility. See our [Gulf payment gateways guide](/en/blog/gulf-payment-gateways).

## Buy Now Pay Later

SAMA's [Rules for Regulating BNPL Companies](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/rules-regulating-buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-companies-0), issued on 17 December 2023 under the Finance Companies Control Law, define a BNPL company as a **joint stock company** licensed by SAMA. The minimum capital is **SAR 5,000,000**, and a licence is valid for **five years**, with renewal requested at least three months before expiry. A BNPL feature is therefore a company structure decision, not a checkout widget.

## The Regulatory Sandbox

SAMA's [Regulatory Sandbox Framework](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/entiresection/1368) is a live testing environment with real customers, for business models or technologies that SAMA's existing rules do not yet cover. What the framework says:

- **Always open.** There are no cohorts or deadlines, so you can apply at any time.
- **Initial response:** SAMA aims to respond to an initial application within **60 business days**.
- **Testing period:** a minimum of **6 months** and a maximum of **12 months** from the Letter of Acceptance.
- **Who can apply:** SAMA-licensed firms; unlicensed local fintechs, either through a Letter of Acceptance or by partnering with a licensed firm; and international fintechs, directly or through a licensed partner.
- **Three exits:** apply for a full licence once SAMA issues the relevant rules; wind down with a final report and customer safeguards; or continue without SAMA permission if SAMA confirms no oversight is needed.

The sandbox is not an exemption. Its scope list includes the [Cyber Security Framework](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/cyber-security-framework-2), so plan reporting and security from the first sprint.

## Open banking

SAMA [issued its Open Banking Framework](https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-us/mediacenter/news/pages/news-794.aspx) in 2022. The first version covers **Account Information Services (AIS)**, which read a customer's account data with consent, and the second covers **Payment Initiation Services (PIS)**, which start a payment from the customer's bank account. The [Open Banking Lab](https://openbanking.sama.gov.sa/index-en.html) gives banks and fintechs a sandbox that simulates bank APIs, with conformance suites to test and certify an implementation.

On **26 March 2026**, SAMA [announced that it had started licensing fintech companies to provide open banking services](https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-US/MediaCenter/News/pages/news-1135.aspx), after the sandbox phase was completed. For a developer this means: if your product reads bank data or initiates payments, there is now a licensing route designed for it, and you should plan to pass the Lab's conformance testing rather than build against a bank's API directly.

## Securities products: the CMA's FinTech Lab

SAMA does not regulate everything. The [CMA's FinTech Lab](https://cma.gov.sa/en/Market/fintech/Pages/default.aspx), launched in 2018, lets firms test financial products and services **related to securities activities** under a FinTech Experimental Permit. Applications are accepted all year and reviewed under the CMA's Financial Technology Experimental Permit Instructions. The CMA has granted these permits for equity crowdfunding platforms ([Mekyal, July 2020](https://cma.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/NEWS/Pages/CMA_N_2756.aspx)) and robo-advisory services ([Sarwa, January 2021](https://cma.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/NEWS/Pages/CMA_N_2840.aspx)). Debt-based and lending products are a different matter, so confirm which regulator applies before you assume the CMA route. If a product mixes payments and investment, expect to talk to both regulators.

## Technical requirements

**Cyber security.** SAMA's [Cyber Security Framework](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/cyber-security-framework-2) (circular 381000091275, 24 May 2017, in force) has four domains: leadership and governance; risk management and compliance; operations and technology; and third-party security. Member organisations must operate at **maturity level 3 ("structured and formalised") or higher**. In practice that means written, approved and evidenced controls, not good intentions.

**Operational resilience.** Tested backups, a continuity plan and monitoring. "Tested" means restored, not just configured.

**Regulatory reporting.** Design your system to produce regulator reports automatically. Pulling them by hand every period is a permanent operating cost.

**Data protection.** The [Personal Data Protection Law](https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/portal/pdp/knowledgecenter/details/PDPL) applies alongside SAMA's rules. Note what it actually says: financial data is not in the law's list of *sensitive* data. **Credit data** (financing applications, credit history, ability to repay) is its own defined category, and Article 24 requires explicit consent before it is collected, repurposed or disclosed. Article 29 restricts transfers outside the Kingdom to listed purposes and conditions. See our [PDPL compliance guide](/en/blog/pdpl-compliance-guide).

**Card data.** Do not store it. Let a licensed gateway tokenise it, which keeps the heaviest card-security obligations with the provider.

## Timeline and budget

| Item | What the rules say |
|---|---|
| Payment licence decision | Within 90 calendar days of SAMA confirming the application is complete |
| Sandbox initial response | Within 60 business days (SAMA's target) |
| Sandbox testing | 6 to 12 months |
| BNPL licence | Valid 5 years; minimum capital SAR 5,000,000 |
| Cyber security | Maturity level 3 or higher, continuously, not once |

None of the frameworks publishes an end-to-end figure from first application to launch, and none counts the time you spend preparing a complete file. Run the regulatory track in parallel with design, not after the build.

**Engineering budget.** On our rate card, a fintech product usually sits in the **advanced app or platform** tier: **$45,000 – $100,000 (SAR 168,800 – 375,000)**, over 5–9 months. **In-app payments** through a licensed provider add **$3,000 – $7,000 (SAR 11,300 – 26,300)**, and each additional local payment gateway **$1,500 – $5,000 (SAR 5,600 – 18,800)**. Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. Licence fees, capital requirements and legal fees are separate and not included.

## Common mistakes

**1. Building first and asking about regulation later.** You may find your business model needs a licence, a capital level or a company structure you cannot reach in your current form.

**2. Assuming "just an interface" exempts you.** If your product touches money or banking data, the rules reach you whatever your technical layer.

**3. Picking the wrong regulator.** An equity crowdfunding or robo-advice product that only talks to SAMA has started in the wrong place.

**4. Ignoring reporting in the design.** Manual compliance reports every period are a cost that good design avoids.

**5. Relying on an unlicensed payment provider.** Check the licence yourself.

**6. Storing card data.** Don't.

## Pre-start checklist

- [ ] Regulator and route confirmed with a specialist advisor (SAMA licence, SAMA sandbox, or CMA FinTech Lab).
- [ ] Licence category identified, including capital and company-structure requirements.
- [ ] Cyber security controls documented to SAMA's maturity level 3.
- [ ] Architecture that produces regulator reports automatically.
- [ ] A continuity plan and restored-from-backup tests.
- [ ] A SAMA-licensed payment provider, licence verified.
- [ ] No card data stored in your system.
- [ ] PDPL obligations mapped, including explicit consent for credit data.
- [ ] Data residency reviewed, including backups and logs.
- [ ] Encryption in transit and at rest, access control, and audit logs for every sensitive operation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a budgeting app that connects to bank accounts need a SAMA licence?

Manual-entry budgeting is usually out of scope. Once the app reads account data from banks, it is providing account information services under SAMA's Open Banking Framework, and SAMA began licensing fintechs for open banking on 26 March 2026. Settle this before you build the bank connection.

### How long does SAMA take to decide a payment licence?

SAMA's guidelines say it will notify its decision within 90 calendar days of confirming the application is complete, after an in-principle approval stage. The time to prepare a complete file is on top of that and depends on you.

### Do I go to SAMA or the CMA?

Payments, e-wallets, open banking, BNPL and other banking activity go to SAMA. Products related to securities, such as equity crowdfunding and robo-advice, go to the CMA, whose FinTech Lab issues experimental permits all year.

### Can an unlicensed startup enter SAMA's sandbox?

Yes. The framework lets unlicensed local fintechs enter with a Letter of Acceptance or through a partnership with a licensed firm, and international fintechs can apply directly or through a licensed partner. Testing then lasts 6 to 12 months.

### Is financial data "sensitive data" under the PDPL?

Not in the law's definition. Sensitive data covers origin, beliefs, security and criminal data, biometric, genetic and health data, and unknown parentage. Credit data is a separate category with its own rule: explicit consent before collection, repurposing or disclosure (Article 24).

### Can a Saudi fintech host its data outside the Kingdom?

That is a question for your advisor and, for a licensed entity, for SAMA, before you choose an architecture. The PDPL allows transfers abroad only for listed purposes and under conditions (Article 29), and your licence or SAMA's outsourcing expectations may add more. See [data residency in the Gulf](/en/blog/data-residency-gulf) and the [hosting comparison](/en/blog/hosting-comparison).

## Where to start

Settle three things before any code: which regulator, which licence or permit, and what that licence requires of your architecture. The published numbers (90 days for a payment licence decision, 6 to 12 months in the sandbox, SAR 5 million capital for BNPL) are enough to plan around, but only once the route is clear.
