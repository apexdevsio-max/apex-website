# Mobile App Development Cost in 2026 — The Real Numbers Guide

**A mobile app costs $5,000–$15,000 if it is simple, $15,000–$45,000 at mid complexity, and $45,000–$100,000 for an advanced app or multi-role platform.** E-commerce apps sit at $20,000–$65,000 and AI-powered apps at $40,000–$150,000 or more. Most business apps land in the mid tier.

Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. The rest of this guide explains which tier your idea falls in, what each feature adds, and what the app costs after launch.

> **Building for Saudi Arabia, the UAE or Qatar?** The prices below already show SAR, AED and QAR. For what is genuinely different in the Gulf (local payment rails such as mada, Arabic interfaces and data protection law), read the **[Gulf app development cost guide](/en/blog/mobile-app-development-cost-gulf)** alongside this one.

| Simple app | $5,000 – $15,000 | 19,000 – 56,500 | 18,500 – 55,000 | 18,000 – 54,500 | 6 – 10 weeks | Product catalogue, booking for one clinic or salon |
| Mid-complexity app | $15,000 – $45,000 | 56,500 – 169,000 | 55,000 – 165,500 | 54,500 – 164,000 | 3 – 5 months | Ordering and delivery for one business, property listings, staff attendance |

Local-currency figures use the fixed pegs (1 USD = 3.75 SAR = 3.6725 AED = 3.64 QAR), rounded. The ranges assume one cross-platform codebase (Flutter or React Native) covering iOS and Android, a back end and an admin panel. If you want to see how we scope a build against these tiers, our [mobile app development service](/en/services/mobile-apps) walks through the process.

**How to place your app in a tier.** Count the user roles and the moving parts. One role with mostly read-only screens is simple. Accounts, payments, an admin panel and one or two integrations make it mid. Several roles that depend on each other in real time (customer, driver, merchant, admin) make it advanced.

A feature that is already part of a tier's definition, such as payments in a mid-tier app, is not charged twice. Live tracking across many merchants and drivers is an advanced platform.

## The five things that move the price

### 1. Complexity and roles

A product catalogue and a delivery platform serving customers, drivers, restaurants and admins are different projects. Every extra role brings its own screens, permissions and test cases, and roles that interact in real time multiply the testing. This is why the tiers are defined by roles and integrations rather than by screen count.

### 2. Platform

Building iOS and Android natively means two codebases and two teams' worth of work. Cross-platform frameworks such as **[Flutter](/en/blog/flutter)** and **[React Native](/en/blog/react-native)** produce both apps from one codebase, which is why the rate-card ranges assume them. Native-only builds are justified for demanding camera, 3D or low-level hardware work, and they cost more than the ranges above.

### 3. Design

A custom interface costs more than a template but gives you a distinct product and fewer compromises later. In Arabic-first products, designing right-to-left from the start is cheaper than mirroring a finished left-to-right design; see the [Gulf guide](/en/blog/mobile-app-development-cost-gulf) for what that involves.

### 4. Who builds it

| Who | Hourly rate |
|---|---|
| Independent freelancer | $15 – $40 |
| Regional or local development agency | $50 – $100 |

The higher rate buys project management, QA, and a team that is still there after launch. A freelancer is a sound choice for a prototype; for an app your business depends on, the comparison is in [freelancer or agency](/en/blog/freelancer-vs-agency).

### 5. Features

See the add-on table above. Payments, live tracking, chat and AI are the features that most often move an app from one tier to the next.

## iOS, Android or both?

![Flutter and React Native producing one app for iOS and Android](/images/blog/mobile-app-development-cost/platform-comparison.jpeg)

- **One platform only:** cheapest and fastest. Choose it only if your own analytics show your customers sit almost entirely on one platform.
- **Cross-platform (Flutter or React Native):** one codebase, two apps. The right default for delivery, booking, e-commerce and internal apps. [Tafawwoq](/en/portfolio/tafawwoq-educational-app), the Arabic education app we built, is a Flutter app with a fully right-to-left interface on both platforms.
- **Native per platform (Swift and Kotlin):** the most expensive route, worth it for performance-critical work such as professional camera tools or 3D.

## How to cut the budget without cutting quality

1. **Launch an MVP.** Ship the one workflow that solves the main problem, then build the rest from real usage. Dropping one add-on, such as in-app chat, removes $5,000–$10,000 from the quote. See [MVP or full product?](/en/blog/mvp-vs-full-product).
2. **Use one cross-platform codebase** unless you have a specific technical reason not to.
3. **Write the requirements down before development.** Changes after coding has started mean rework on design, code and tests at once; a scoped document is the cheapest protection you can buy.
4. **Do not buy on the lowest quote.** Compare the three-year cost (below), not the launch price.
5. **Match the team to the stakes.** Prototypes suit freelancers; core business apps need structured QA and post-launch support.

## What a professional quote should include

- **Scope of work:** every screen and feature named, not described in general terms.
- **Cost by phase:** design, development, QA and release priced separately.
- **Milestone dates:** a date per phase, not "about three months".
- **Supported platforms and OS versions.**
- **Source code ownership:** full ownership transfers to you on payment.
- **Warranty:** how many months of bug fixes after launch, and what they cover.
- **Change policy:** how changes are estimated and billed.
- **Communication:** your point of contact, the tracking tool and the reporting rhythm.

A missing item is a question to ask before signing, not a detail to fix later.

## After launch: maintenance and running costs

![Annual maintenance as part of the total cost of a mobile app](/images/blog/mobile-app-development-cost/maintenance-cost.jpeg)

**Budget 15–20% of the build cost every year for maintenance.** An app that cost $40,000 to build needs $6,000–$8,000 a year (SAR 22,500–30,000 / AED 22,000–29,500 / QAR 22,000–29,000) for OS compatibility updates, bug fixes and security patches.

Two store fees are fixed and published: the [Apple Developer Program costs 99 USD a year](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/), and [Google Play charges a one-time US$25 registration fee](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en). Hosting, SMS, maps and analytics are billed by each provider according to usage, so get those prices from the providers you choose before you commit.

## How you pay: fixed price, time and materials, or retainer

| Model | How it works | You carry the risk of | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed price** | One agreed total for an agreed scope | The scope being wrong | Requirements are genuinely settled |
| **Time and materials** | Billed per hour or per sprint | Cost overrun | Scope will evolve as you learn |
| **Retainer** | A fixed monthly amount for ongoing capacity | Paying for idle capacity | Continuous development after launch |
| **Milestone-based** | Payment released per delivered stage | Mostly balanced | Most commercial projects |

**Fixed price is not automatically safe.** The vendor carries the scope risk, so it prices that risk into the quote, and every change becomes a negotiation. **Time and materials is not a blank cheque** if you cap each sprint and agree a written change threshold.

**What we recommend:** a fixed scope with milestone payments for a well-defined first phase, then time and materials for what comes after.

**Payment schedule red flags:**
- More than 40% requested upfront
- No deliverable tied to any payment
- Final payment due before you have tested anything
- Source code released only after the final payment clears; [App Development Contracts](/en/blog/app-development-contract) explains why that clause matters

## The three-year cost of a $30,000 app

A mid-complexity app with a $30,000 build (SAR 112,500 / AED 110,000 / QAR 109,000), over its first three years after launch:

| Item | Three-year cost |
|---|---|
| Build (one-off) | $30,000 |
| Maintenance at 15–20% a year, three years | $13,500 – $18,000 |
| Apple Developer Program, 3 × $99 | $297 |
| Google Play registration, once | $25 |
| **Known total** | **$43,822 – $48,322** (SAR 164,500–181,000 / AED 161,000–177,500 / QAR 159,500–176,000) |
| Hosting, SMS, maps, analytics | Usage-priced by each provider |
| New features | Priced per feature, e.g. in-app chat $5,000–$10,000 |

Even before hosting and new features, the build is only about two thirds of the three-year spend. Two consequences follow:

1. **Maintenance is not optional.** Apple and Google change their operating systems and store requirements every year. Skipping a year of maintenance turns into a bigger repair bill later.
2. **New features are the largest variable.** A successful app attracts feature requests; budget for them, using the add-on prices above.

For how to staff that ongoing work, see [In-House Maintenance or a Contract?](/en/blog/maintenance-inhouse-vs-contract).

## Timeline and team

| Tier | Timeline | Typical team |
|---|---|---|
| Simple app | 6 – 10 weeks | 1 developer, 1 designer, part-time PM |
| Mid-complexity | 3 – 5 months | 2–3 developers, designer, QA, PM |
| Advanced platform | 5 – 9 months | 4–6 developers, designer, QA, PM |

The schedule runs through discovery, design, development, testing and store release. Development is the biggest phase but not the whole schedule, and adding developers does not shorten it in proportion: coordination grows with team size, and design has to come before the screens that implement it.

**What actually delays projects:**
1. Slow feedback and approvals on the client side, the one delay entirely in your control
2. Scope changes mid-build
3. Third-party integrations behaving differently from their documentation
4. App Store rejections: [Apple reviews 90% of submissions in under 48 hours](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/), but each rejection adds a fix-and-resubmit cycle
5. Content and assets arriving late

Nominate one decision-maker with authority to approve, and write a response window into the contract.

## Frequently asked questions

### What makes an app "mid-complexity", and what does that tier cost?

User accounts, payments, an admin panel and one or two integrations with one main user role. As of November 2026 that tier costs $15,000–$45,000 (SAR 56,500–169,000 / AED 55,000–165,500 / QAR 54,500–164,000) and takes 3–5 months.

### When does a feature push my app into the next tier instead of adding to the price?

When it adds a role that depends on others in real time. Push notifications add $1,500–$3,000 to a simple app. A driver role with live tracking that customers and merchants watch turns the product into an advanced platform at $45,000–$100,000.

### Is one Flutter codebase for iOS and Android cheaper than two native apps?

Yes. One codebase means one team building and testing once for both stores, which is why every range in our rate card assumes Flutter or React Native. Native builds cost more and are worth it only for performance-critical work.

### How much should I set aside each year after the app launches?

15–20% of the build cost for maintenance, plus $99 a year for Apple's developer programme, and hosting and third-party services priced by usage. Google Play's $25 fee is paid once.

### Is a freelancer at $15–$40 an hour cheaper than an agency at $50–$100?

Per hour, yes. Over the life of a business-critical app, not necessarily: an agency's rate includes project management, QA and post-launch support that you otherwise organise and pay for yourself.

### Why does a $30,000 app cost $44,000–$48,000 over three years?

Maintenance at 15–20% a year adds $13,500–$18,000 over three years, and store fees add $322. Hosting and new features come on top of that figure.
