# Template or Custom Design? And Its Effect on Conversion

**A well-chosen template is enough for most business websites. Custom design earns its cost when visual identity is part of what you sell, when the site is your main source of revenue, or when you need an experience no template supports.** The sharper question isn't "which looks better?" but **"which turns visitors into customers?"** — and on that, speed and message clarity usually matter more than the design itself.

## What actually affects conversion

Here lies the biggest misunderstanding on this subject. **Design beauty is not the largest factor in conversion.** The bigger factors, in order:

**1. Speed.** Visitors leave a slow page before seeing its design. Google states that [Core Web Vitals are used by its ranking systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience), and sets "good" at a Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, an Interaction to Next Paint under 200 milliseconds, and a Cumulative Layout Shift below 0.1 ([Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)). A light template beats a heavy custom design — and effect-rich templates are often the slowest of all.

**2. Message clarity.** Does the visitor understand what you offer within seconds? That is copy, not design.

**3. Call-to-action clarity.** A visible button with specific text ("Get a free estimate") beats an elegant button with vague text ("Discover more").

**4. Trust.** Real work, clear contact information, verifiable testimonials. **Real photos of your team beat any stock image however professional** — because visitors recognise stock photography.

**5. Mobile usability.** Google [uses the mobile version of a site's content for indexing and ranking](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing), and your own analytics will show what share of your visits come from phones. A design that works flawlessly on a small screen matters more than any aesthetic detail.

**6. Visual design.** Important — but after all of the above.

**The practical conclusion:** if your conversion rate is low, design is rarely the primary cause. Investigate speed and message clarity first.

## When a template suffices

- **A brochure site** for a service company or professional office.
- **A blog or content site.**
- **An online store at the start** — Salla and Shopify themes have already been used by many live stores.
- **Testing an idea** before major investment.
- **A limited budget** — a good template is far better than a poor custom design.

**An overlooked advantage:** widely used templates have been exercised on many live sites, so their button and form placements reflect what has worked in practice, not one designer's taste.

---

## When custom design earns its cost

**1. Visual identity is part of your value.** A fashion brand, an upmarket restaurant, a creative studio — here resembling thousands of other sites genuinely hurts.

**2. The site is your primary revenue source.** If most of your customers arrive through it, a small conversion improvement recovers the design cost quickly.

**3. An unconventional experience.** An interactive price calculator, a complex booking flow, a configurable product — templates don't support these.

**4. Complex Arabic RTL requirements.** Many global templates break their layout when converted to Arabic. A design built for Arabic from the start avoids this.

> **A practical warning:** if you choose a template for an Arabic site, **test it in Arabic before purchase, not after**. Many templates claim RTL support and break their layout in actual use.

## The middle path — best for most cases

An overlooked option that is usually the smartest: **a strong template as a foundation + deep identity customisation**.

- You keep a tested, fast structure.
- You change colours, typography, imagery, and icons to reflect your identity.
- You reorder sections according to your priorities.
- **Cost:** the project stays in its rate-card tier instead of climbing to the top of it — a small site stays within $1,500–5,000 (as of November 2026).

**The result:** a site that doesn't look like a stock template, with a template's performance and reliability.

## Signs of a poor template

Not all templates are equal. Avoid one that:

- **Is slow** — test the demo with a speed measurement tool before buying.
- **Is loaded with features you don't need** — every feature slows the site.
- **Doesn't genuinely support RTL** — test in Arabic, not by reading the description.
- **Is old or unmaintained** — check the last update date.
- **Depends on many plugins** — each is a failure point and a security update.

## How to choose a good template

If you decide on a template, choosing it deserves an hour of checking:

**1. Test the demo's speed** with a measurement tool such as PageSpeed Insights before buying — don't rely on your visual impression. Compare the result with the [Core Web Vitals thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/vitals).

**2. Open it on your own phone.** Many templates look excellent on a large screen and stumble on mobile.

**3. Test it in Arabic.** Add Arabic text and look at the mirrored layout. This single test rules out a lot of templates.

**4. Check the last update date.** A template not updated in two years is an abandoned template.

**5. Read buyer reviews** — especially the negative ones, which reveal the real problems.

**6. Count the plugins it requires.** A template that needs ten plugins to work has ten potential points of failure.

**7. Make sure the licence is clear** — does it allow commercial use, and does it include future updates?

## Frequently asked questions

### Does custom design actually raise conversion?

Not necessarily, which surprises many people. Design is one factor among several — speed, message clarity, and call-to-action clarity usually outrank it in impact. Custom design raises conversion when it solves a specific experience problem, not merely by being custom.

### How do I know if my site needs custom design?

Ask: does my site resemble competitors in a way that hurts? Is the experience I need unavailable in templates? Is visual identity part of my brand's value? If the answer is no to all three, a template will serve you.

### Can a template be customised to look unique?

Yes, substantially. Changing colours, typography, imagery, and section order produces a site that doesn't look like a stock template. This is the middle path, and for a small site it stays within the $1,500–5,000 tier (as of November 2026).

### What is the biggest mistake in choosing a template for an Arabic site?

Choosing it from a beautiful English demo without testing its speed and its Arabic layout. An effect-heavy template looks impressive in the demo and becomes sluggish with your real content, and many break when mirrored for RTL. Test speed and Arabic on a phone before buying.

### Are stock photos acceptable?

For backgrounds and generic elements: yes. For photos of your team, premises, and work: **no**. Visitors recognise stock photography, and it weakens trust at the moment you need it most. A simple photo session beats the best stock image.

### Which price tier does a template-based site fall into?

The same tier as a custom-designed site of the same size — the template just keeps it at the lower end. On our rate card (as of November 2026) that is $1,500–5,000 for a landing page or small brochure site and $8,000–25,000 for a mid-size company website. A bespoke experience such as a booking flow moves the project into the custom web application tier, $25,000–80,000.

## The short version

**A template suffices in most cases** — and widely used templates have been proven on many live sites, which is an advantage rather than a flaw.

**Custom design earns its cost** when identity is part of your value, the site is your revenue source, or the experience is unconventional.

**The middle path offers the best value** — a strong template with deep identity customisation.

**Most importantly:** if your conversion is low, investigate speed and message clarity before blaming the design.
