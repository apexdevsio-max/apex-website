# Template or Custom Design? And Its Effect on Conversion

The gap between a ready-made template and custom design can reach ten times in price — which makes the question legitimate. But the sharper question isn't "which looks better?" so much as **"which converts visitors into customers?"**

**The short answer: a template suffices in most cases. Custom design earns its cost when visual identity is part of your value, or when conversion is your primary revenue source.**

---

| Approach | Cost | Timeline |

## What actually affects conversion

Here lies the biggest misunderstanding on this subject. **Design beauty is not the largest factor in conversion.** The bigger factors, in order:

**1. Speed.** Visitors leave a slow page before seeing its design — and it is a [stated ranking factor](https://web.dev/articles/vitals). A light template beats a heavy custom design — and effect-rich templates are often slower.

**2. Message clarity.** Does the visitor understand what you offer within seconds? That is copy, not design.

**3. Call-to-action clarity.** A visible button with specific text ("Get a free estimate") beats an elegant button with vague text ("Discover more").

**4. Trust.** Real work, clear contact information, verifiable testimonials. **Real photos of your team beat any stock image however professional** — because visitors recognise stock photography immediately.

**5. Mobile usability.** Most of your visitors are on mobile. A design that works flawlessly on a small screen matters more than any aesthetic detail.

**6. Visual design.** Important — but after all of the above.

**The practical conclusion:** if your conversion rate is low, design is rarely the primary cause. Investigate speed and message clarity first.

## When a template suffices

- **A brochure site** for a service company or professional office.
- **A blog or content site.**
- **An online store at the start** — Salla and Shopify themes are tested across thousands of stores.
- **Testing an idea** before major investment.
- **A limited budget** — a good template is far better than a poor custom design.

**An overlooked advantage:** successful templates are tested across thousands of sites — their patterns for button and form placement are built on what actually worked, not on a designer's taste.

---

## When custom design earns its cost

**1. Visual identity is part of your value.** A fashion brand, an upmarket restaurant, a creative studio — here resembling thousands of other sites genuinely hurts.

**2. The site is your primary revenue source.** If most of your customers arrive through it, a small conversion improvement recovers the design cost quickly.

**3. An unconventional experience.** An interactive price calculator, a complex booking flow, a configurable product — templates don't support these.

**4. Complex Arabic RTL requirements.** Many global templates break their layout when converted to Arabic. A design built for Arabic from the start avoids this.

> **A practical warning:** if you choose a template for an Arabic site, **test it in Arabic before purchase, not after**. Many templates claim RTL support and break their layout in actual use.

---

## The middle path — best for most cases

An overlooked option that is usually the smartest: **a strong template as a foundation + deep identity customisation**.

- You keep a tested, fast structure.
- You change colours, typography, imagery, and icons to reflect your identity.
- You reorder sections according to your priorities.
- **Cost: $1,500 – $5,000** — close to a template, with a result close to custom.

**The result:** a site that doesn't look like a stock template, with a template's performance and reliability, at a third of full custom cost.

---

## Signs of a poor template

Not all templates are equal. Avoid one that:

- **Is slow** — test the demo with a speed measurement tool before buying.
- **Is loaded with features you don't need** — every feature slows the site.
- **Doesn't genuinely support RTL** — test in Arabic, not by reading the description.
- **Is old or unmaintained** — check the last update date.
- **Depends on many plugins** — each is a failure point and a security update.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does custom design actually raise conversion?**

Not necessarily, which surprises many people. Design is one factor among several — speed, message clarity, and call-to-action clarity usually outrank it in impact. Custom design raises conversion when it solves a specific experience problem, not merely by being custom.

**How do I know if my site needs custom design?**

Ask: does my site resemble competitors in a way that hurts? Is the experience I need unavailable in templates? Is visual identity part of my brand's value? If the answer is no to all three, a template will serve you.

**Can a template be customised to look unique?**

Yes, substantially. Changing colours, typography, imagery, and section order produces a site that doesn't look like a stock template. This is the middle path, and it suits most cases at $1,500–$5,000.

**What is the biggest mistake in choosing a template?**

Choosing it based on a beautiful demo without testing its speed and Arabic support. An effect-heavy template looks impressive in the demo and becomes sluggish with your real content. Test speed and Arabic before buying.

**Are stock photos acceptable?**

For backgrounds and generic elements: yes. For photos of your team, premises, and work: **no**. Visitors recognise stock photography immediately, and it weakens trust at the moment you need it most. A simple photo session beats the best stock image.

**How much do I save with a template?**

Between 60% and 90% of custom design cost. But calculate total cost: a poor template needs fixes and revisions that can consume the difference. A good template, deeply customised, is the best value-per-cost ratio for most projects.

## Conclusion

**A template suffices in most cases** — and successful templates are tested across thousands of sites, which is an advantage rather than a flaw.

**Custom design earns its cost** when identity is part of your value, the site is your revenue source, or the experience is unconventional.

**And the middle path offers the best value-per-cost** — a strong template with deep identity customisation.

**Most importantly:** if your conversion is low, investigate speed and message clarity before blaming the design.
