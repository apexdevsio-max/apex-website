# Mobile App or Responsive Website (PWA)? Which Do You Actually Need

**Start with a responsive website or progressive web app (PWA), and build a mobile app only if at least one of five conditions holds: customers use you weekly or more, the service must work offline, push notifications to iPhone users are essential, you need deep hardware access, or the app itself is your product.** If none applies, a website reaches more people, appears in search and costs less to run.

On our published rate card (as of November 2026), a mid-size company website is **$8,000–$25,000** and a simple app **$5,000–$15,000** — but the app adds store accounts, commission on digital sales, store review on every release, and the cost of persuading people to download it.

---

## What is a PWA?

MDN defines a **[progressive web app](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps)** as "an app that's built using web platform technologies, but that provides a user experience like that of a platform-specific app." Like a website it runs on any device from one codebase; like an app it "can be installed on the device, can operate while offline and in the background, and can integrate with the device."

In short: **much of an app's value, without a store, a download, or a review process.**

The main iPhone caveat: Apple added web push notifications in [iOS and iPadOS 16.4](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/) (announced February 2023), and only for web apps the user has added to the Home Screen, with permission requested after a direct user action such as tapping a subscribe button.

## The download barrier — the biggest overlooked factor

Every step between a customer's interest and their first use loses some of the audience: they see the link → open the store → wait for the download → grant permissions → create an account. A website removes the middle three steps.

**The decisive question:** is your app's value worth downloading for someone who hasn't tried you yet?

- **A restaurant a customer orders from twice a month** → they are unlikely to download. They will order from a website or a delivery platform.
- **A bank they use daily** → they will download without hesitation.

**And discovery:** your website's pages can rank in Google search; an app's screens cannot — only its store listing can be found. If acquiring customers through search is part of your plan, an app alone gives that up.

---

## Five reasons — and only these — that justify building an app

If none apply, a responsive website will serve you:

**1. Repeat usage by the same customer.** Daily, or weekly at minimum: banking, fitness, productivity, communication. Repeat usage justifies the one-time download effort.

**2. A genuine need for offline operation.** A field app for technicians in areas without coverage, or a travel app. "The connection might drop sometimes" is not sufficient reason — a PWA can cache key pages.

**3. Reliable push notifications essential to the service.** Alerting a driver to a new order, a security alert. On iPhone, web push needs iOS 16.4 or later and the user must first add your site to the Home Screen — an extra step you cannot count on. If your audience is on iPhone and the service depends on notifications, that is a genuine reason for an app.

**4. Deep device hardware access.** Camera with real-time processing, Bluetooth peripherals, biometric sensors, continuous background location tracking.

**5. The app itself is your product.** If you sell the app or earn from within it, being in the stores is part of your business model.

## When a website clearly suffices

- **Presenting services and collecting enquiries** — service companies, clinics, professional offices.
- **An online store** — most stores can sell over the web. See our [e-commerce cost guide](/en/blog/ecommerce-development-cost-saudi).
- **Content and blogs** — appearing in search matters far more than an icon on a screen.
- **Simple appointment booking** — a web booking flow falls in the company-website or custom-web-application tier, depending on integrations. See our [clinic app guide](/en/blog/clinic-booking-app-cost).
- **A brochure site** — no debate here.

## The smarter path: start with web

**1. Build an excellent responsive website first** — usually the mid-size company website tier, $8,000–$25,000. Launch within weeks, appear in search, gather real users.

**2. Measure behaviour.** How many users return weekly? How many ask for notifications? What share of visitors are on mobile, and on which platform?

**3. Build the app when the numbers demand it** — with requirements grounded in actual behaviour rather than assumptions. When you get there, [Native vs Hybrid Apps](/en/blog/native-vs-hybrid-app) and [Flutter vs React Native](/en/blog/flutter-vs-react-native) cover the technology choice.

**Why is this smarter?** You test demand on the cheaper channel, and build the app later knowing what people actually use — rather than building features nobody turns out to want.

---

## Common mistakes in this decision

**Building an app because a competitor did.** The competitor may be wrong, or have a different business model. Your decision follows your own customers' behaviour.

**Measuring success by downloads.** A download says nothing about use. Track how many users come back each week; that is the number that justifies an app.

**Assuming an app brings new customers.** An app serves people who already know you; a website reaches people who don't, through search. Building an app without a website cuts off your main acquisition channel.

**Ignoring the marketing cost.** Building the app is half the equation; getting people to install it is the other half. Budget for it before you budget the build.

**Building both at once from the start.** That doubles cost and effort before you know whether either works. Start with one, prove demand, then expand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a PWA send push notifications to iPhone users?

Yes, but only on iOS and iPadOS 16.4 or later, and only after the user has added your web app to the Home Screen and granted permission in response to a direct action such as tapping a button. You cannot count on visitors taking that Home Screen step, which is why iPhone-heavy audiences that depend on notifications are a genuine reason to build an app.

### What does an app cost each year that a website doesn't?

Apple's developer program is $99 per membership year and Google Play has a one-time $25 registration fee. If you sell digital goods or subscriptions in the app, the stores take a commission: 15% for smaller developers on both stores, and 30% on Google Play revenue above $1M a year. Every update also goes through store review rather than going live instantly.

### Can a PWA be found through Google search?

Yes — it is a website, so each page can be indexed and rank like any other. An app's content cannot be found in Google the same way; people find it through its store listing or a link you send them.

### Do I need both a website and an app?

Many companies end up with both — a website for discovery and search, an app for repeat customers. But **start with the website**: it serves your whole audience, while the app serves a loyal segment that does not exist yet if you are just starting.

### What should I measure before moving from web to an app?

Weekly return rate, repeated requests for features only an app can deliver well (reliable notifications, offline use, background location), and the share of your mobile visitors on iPhone. If returning users are a small minority, an app will not change that.

### Can I turn my PWA into a store app later?

You can, but think of it as a new build rather than a conversion if you need deep hardware access or native notifications. What carries over is your backend, your content and, most valuably, the usage data that tells you which features the app actually needs.
