# App Development in Jeddah: Pricing and Available Options (2026)

Jeddah is a commercial market with a different character from Riyadh. Its economy is more tied to trade, imports, logistics, and family-owned business — and that shapes directly the kind of apps companies here actually need.

This guide gives you realistic 2026 pricing, the execution options available, and what sets Jeddah projects apart.

## What makes Jeddah projects distinct?

**Trade and imports.** Port activity and commerce create high demand for inventory management, shipment tracking, and supplier integration apps. These are integration-heavy by nature — their value lies in connecting to existing systems rather than in their interface.

**Family businesses and mid-size firms.** Many Jeddah companies are family enterprises with entrenched operating systems. An app here is rarely built from scratch; more often it is built **on top of an existing system** — which makes integration the most complex line item in the project and the one that most affects the timeline.

**Logistics and delivery.** Proximity to the port and goods movement create demand for delivery and real-time tracking apps. See our [delivery app cost guide](/en/blog/delivery-app-cost) if that is your field.

**Seasonality.** Hajj and Umrah seasons create sharp demand peaks for hospitality, transport, and service apps. Any app serving that sector needs **architecture that withstands sudden load spikes** — an architectural decision made at design time, not added later, and one that affects cost.

## Requirements that add to your budget

**Local payment gateways.** Mada is the most widely used payment card in Saudi Arabia, joined by Apple Pay, STC Pay, and buy-now-pay-later options. Budget **$1,500 – $5,000 per gateway**.

**Localisation and RTL support.** Not merely translating text, but mirrored interface layouts, font handling, and testing across device sizes. **$2,000 – $8,000**, and the figure rises considerably if added after design rather than accounted for from the start.

**Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL).** Enforcement has been active since September 2024 under [SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/), covering explicit consent, breach notification within 72 hours, and restrictions on transferring data outside the Kingdom. Baseline compliance is **$1,000 – $3,000**, rising for sensitive data.

**Store fees.** Apple: $99 annually · Google: a one-time $25 registration fee.

## How to choose

Five questions reveal the difference before signing:

1. **Show me two published apps you built end to end** — store links, not screenshots.
2. **Who will write the code — your employees or contractors?**
3. **How will you integrate with my existing system?** — a decisive question in Jeddah specifically.
4. **Do the code and store accounts belong entirely to me?**
5. **Can I speak to two former clients?**

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does app development cost in Jeddah?**

Between $8,000 for a simple MVP and over $120,000 for an enterprise platform. Most mid-size business apps — with Arabic support and payment integration — fall between $25,000 and $60,000. Pricing tracks Riyadh closely, and the real difference comes from project scope rather than city.

**Should I choose a Jeddah firm or an external team?**

What actually matters is time-zone overlap and local market understanding, not the address. A team working in a nearby time zone that understands Mada, localisation, and PDPL operates more efficiently than one ten hours away. Always verify **who will actually write the code**, as many firms subcontract execution.

**How long does app development take?**

A scoped MVP: 6–10 weeks. A mid-size app with a backend: 3–5 months. An enterprise platform: 9–18 months. In Jeddah projects specifically, **integrating with an existing system** is the most common source of delay — so document your system's interfaces before requesting any quote.

**What apps are most in demand in the Jeddah market?**

Inventory management, shipment tracking, and supplier integration apps, driven by trade and port activity. Next are delivery and logistics apps, then hospitality and transport apps tied to the Hajj and Umrah seasons.

**Do I need an app, or is a website enough?**

If your goal is presenting services or selling products, a responsive website is faster, cheaper, and usually sufficient. An app earns its cost when you need push notifications, offline operation, repeat usage, or device capabilities. See our [website design cost guide](/en/blog/website-design-cost) for the comparison.

**How do I prepare for Hajj and Umrah peak season?**

If your app serves that sector, load-bearing architecture is a design-stage decision, not a later addition. Discuss the expected peak load scenario with your provider, and request load testing well before the season — fixing a crash during peak is far more expensive than preparing for it.
