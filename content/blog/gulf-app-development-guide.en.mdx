# Hiring an App or Web Development Team for Saudi Arabia, the UAE or Qatar

**The price of an app for the Gulf follows its scope, not the city the team sits in: a mid-complexity app is $15,000–$45,000 (SAR 56,500–169,000 / AED 55,000–165,500 / QAR 54,500–164,000) as of November 2026.** What changes from one country to the next is the law the product must follow and the payment rails it must accept: Saudi Arabia's PDPL and ZATCA e-invoicing, the UAE's federal data law with its separate DIFC and ADGM regimes and an e-invoicing mandate starting in 2027, and Qatar's Law No. 13 of 2016 with its separate QFC regime. A team that knows those rules can build for Riyadh, Jeddah, Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Doha from anywhere in the region's time zone.

A word on where this comes from. We are a small software company based in Syria, working remotely with clients in Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar. We have no office in any of these cities, so this guide is written from the remote-team side of the table, and it says plainly where a local firm is the better choice. Our [mobile app development](/en/services/mobile-apps) and [web development](/en/services/web-development) pages explain how we scope a Gulf project.

### Saudi Arabia

**Data protection.** The [PDPL](https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/portal/pdp/knowledgecenter/details/PDPL) applies to processing of personal data in the Kingdom, including processing of data about people residing there by parties outside it (Article 2). Two articles matter when you hire a vendor. Article 8 requires you, as the controller, to choose a processor that gives the necessary safeguards and to check that it complies, so your contract with the development team should say how it handles data. Article 29 sets the conditions for transferring data outside the Kingdom, which bears directly on where the app is hosted. The [PDPL compliance guide](/en/blog/pdpl-compliance-guide) and [data residency in the Gulf](/en/blog/data-residency-gulf) cover both.

**E-invoicing.** [ZATCA's Fatoora programme](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/Introduction/Pages/What-is-e-invoicing.aspx) moved invoices to structured electronic files: the generation phase took effect on 4 December 2021, and the integration phase, which connects invoicing systems to ZATCA, has been rolled out in waves since 1 January 2023. If your app or store issues tax invoices, the invoicing part must be a compliant solution, and the team should tell you which wave you fall in. See [ZATCA e-invoicing for online stores](/en/blog/zatca-einvoicing-guide).

**Payments.** [mada](https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-US/payment/Pages/mada.aspx) is a national payment system owned by the Saudi Central Bank, and mada cards pay online through 3-D Secure. [sarie](https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-US/payment/pages/sarie.aspx), SAMA's instant-payment system launched in 2021, settles transfers of up to SAR 20,000 around the clock and accepts a mobile number, ID number or email in place of an IBAN. Fintech products fall under SAMA's own rules; see [fintech and SAMA compliance](/en/blog/fintech-sama-compliance).

### United Arab Emirates

**Data protection.** [Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws) took effect on 2 January 2022, and the UAE Data Office is the federal data regulator. Two financial free zones run their own regimes: the DIFC under DIFC Law No. 5 of 2020, and ADGM under its [Data Protection Regulations 2021](https://assets.adgm.com/download/assets/ADGM+Data+Protection+Regulations+2021+Updated.pdf/146aa34858b011efb99a36e29b0f3a63), supervised by ADGM's Office of Data Protection. Confirm which regime your licence falls under before the data model is designed; the [UAE data protection guide](/en/blog/uae-data-protection-guide) explains the federal law.

**E-invoicing.** The [Ministry of Finance](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-the-issuance-of-two-ministerial-decisions-on-the-scope-of-obligations-and-the-timelines-for-implementing-the-electronic-invoicing-system-2/) set the system to cover all B2B and B2G transactions, with a pilot from 1 July 2026. Businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more go live on 1 January 2027; [Ministerial Decision No. 66 of 2026](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Ministerial-Resolution-No.-66-of-2026-Amending-Certain-Provisions-of-Ministerial-Resolution-No.-244-of-2025-Regarding-the-Implementation-of-the-Electronic-Invoicing-System-En-20260514.pdf) moved their deadline to appoint an accredited service provider to 30 October 2026. Businesses below that threshold appoint one by 31 March 2027 and go live on 1 July 2027, and in-scope government entities go live on 1 October 2027. If your platform issues invoices, plan the integration with your accredited provider now.

**Payments.** [Jaywan](https://centralbank.ae/media/2crjrusv/jaywan-the-uae-s-first-domestic-card-scheme-en.pdf) is the UAE's first domestic card scheme, run by Al Etihad Payments, a subsidiary of the Central Bank of the UAE, and is accepted at ATMs, point of sale and online. Banks issue Jaywan-only cards as well as cards co-badged with international networks, so ask your gateway whether it accepts the Jaywan-only kind. The same company runs [Aani](https://aep.ae/en/news-media/press-releasesarticles/aani-and-jaywan-the-uaes-bold-leap-towards-a-less-cash-payment-ecosystem/), the instant-payment platform launched in late 2023. Health apps carry extra rules; see [health apps in the UAE](/en/blog/health-apps-uae-compliance).

### Qatar

**Data protection.** Two regimes run in parallel. Most entities fall under Law No. 13 of 2016 on personal data privacy protection, overseen by the [Personal Data Privacy Protection Office at the National Cyber Security Agency](https://www.qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2023-01/30/0041-director-of-national-cyber-governance-affairs-at-ncsa-highlights-role-of-qatar-in-protecting-personal-data-privacy), which issues guidelines and handles complaints. Entities registered in the Qatar Financial Centre follow the [QFC Data Protection Regulations 2021](https://www.qfc.qa/en/operating-with-qfc/data-protection), supervised by the QFC's independent Data Protection Office. Building for one regime and then discovering your entity sits in the other means rework in consent flows and data handling, so settle it before the build.

**Payments.** The Qatar Central Bank [launched Fawran in March 2024](https://www.qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2024-03/07/0036-qcb-launches-'fawran'-instant-payment-service): instant transfers around the clock, using a mobile number or alias instead of an IBAN, capped at QR 50,000 per transaction. It matters if you collect payments by transfer rather than by card. For gateway choices across the region, see [payment gateways in the Gulf](/en/blog/gulf-payment-gateways).

## Hiring a remote regional team: what you gain and what you must protect

A remote team in the region is a real option, not a compromise, as long as you know what it changes.

**What you gain.** Syria, Saudi Arabia and Qatar all keep UTC+3 year-round, and the UAE is one hour ahead, so a team in the region overlaps your whole working day. Arabic is the team's first language, which matters most in right-to-left interfaces: [Tafawwoq](/en/portfolio/tafawwoq-educational-app), the Arabic education app we built in Flutter, has a fully right-to-left interface on iOS and Android from one codebase. And the hourly band is the same $50–$100 a regional agency charges, without a local office's overhead added on.

**What you give up.** In-person workshops are not the default; you meet on video and see working software at a fixed rhythm instead. A dispute with a vendor in another country is slower and costlier to enforce. Public-sector tenders set their own eligibility rules, which may require local registration, so read them before you shortlist a remote team.

**How to protect yourself.** Put the safeguards in the contract rather than in trust:

- The code lives in a repository your company owns from day one, with the team as invited collaborators.
- Apple and Google developer accounts, the domain and the cloud account are registered to your company.
- Payment follows milestones you can test, never a large sum up front.
- The contract names the governing law and the venue for disputes, and states how the team handles personal data, which is what PDPL Article 8 asks of you.

The [app development contract guide](/en/blog/app-development-contract) covers each clause.

**When a local firm is the better choice:** government work that requires a locally registered supplier, projects that need frequent on-site sessions with operations staff, and buyers who will not sign without a local entity they can take to court.

## Sectors and the requirements they bring

The sector shapes the scope more than the city does. These are the patterns to plan for before you ask for a quote.

**Trade, import and logistics.** Apps for inventory, shipment tracking and supplier links are integration projects: their value is in the connection to systems you already run, and that integration is usually the line that moves the timeline most. If delivery is your field, see the [delivery app cost guide](/en/blog/delivery-app-cost).

**Seasonal hospitality, transport and events.** Hajj and Umrah in Saudi Arabia, and large international events in Qatar, create sharp, short peaks. An app serving them needs an architecture that absorbs a sudden surge, and that is a design decision taken at the start, not something added later.

**Government and semi-government services.** Service portals, appointment systems and internal dashboards come with documentation, accessibility and Arabic as requirements rather than options, and with review and approval cycles longer than the private sector's.

**Health.** Patient, booking and records apps meet sector rules that differ between countries: what applies in the UAE does not carry over to Saudi Arabia. See the [clinic booking app cost guide](/en/blog/clinic-booking-app-cost).

**Fintech.** Payments and lending products answer to SAMA in Saudi Arabia, and to their own regulators inside the ADGM, DIFC and QFC free zones, with security and compliance work far beyond an ordinary app.

**Energy and industry.** Field apps for operations and maintenance crews, asset tracking and monitoring dashboards connect to industrial systems and carry stricter security requirements than usual.

## Common mistakes

**Asking for a price before documenting your current system.** Most business apps in the region sit on top of an existing system. If you do not know whether yours can export and receive data through an API, the first estimate falls apart at the first technical call. Write down what your system can send and receive, even in plain language.

**Assuming the third party will cooperate.** If the app must connect to a supplier, a shipping company or an accounting system, confirm that the other side offers the interface you need, and will let you use it, before you sign.

**Ignoring the season.** Launching a Hajj and Umrah app two weeks before the season is a large risk. Launch at least two months ahead to leave time for field testing and fixes.

**Leaving Arabic until last.** Adding right-to-left support after the design is finished costs several times more than planning it from the start, and the result reads as translated rather than designed. Machine translation is spotted immediately, and in government work weak Arabic can be enough for rejection.

**Building for the wrong legal framework.** Designing for federal law and then finding the entity is licensed in ADGM, DIFC or the QFC, or the reverse, means reworking the data model and consent flows.

**Planning to private-sector timelines for public-sector work.** Government and semi-government projects pass through several review rounds. Build that margin into the schedule from the start.

**Deciding hosting late.** Data-transfer rules can dictate where the app is hosted. Answer that question before choosing the technical architecture, not after.

**Choosing the cheapest quote without counting maintenance.** Compare three years: the build, plus maintenance at 15–20% of the build cost a year, plus the changes you already expect.

## Ten questions to ask any team, local or remote

1. Who will actually write the code: your employees or subcontractors?
2. Show me two published products you built end to end, with live links.
3. Which data protection regime applies to my entity, and how will the design handle it?
4. Which local payment rails will you integrate (mada, Jaywan, Fawran), and through which gateway?
5. If the app issues invoices, how will it meet ZATCA or UAE e-invoicing requirements?
6. Where will the data be hosted, and why there?
7. How do you handle change requests during the build?
8. Will the code repository, store accounts and domain be in my company's name from day one?
9. What does the warranty cover, and what does maintenance cost after it ends?
10. Can I speak to two former clients?

A team that hesitates on questions 1, 8 and 10 is the one to avoid. For the full evaluation framework, read [how to choose a development company](/en/blog/choosing-development-company), and to define the scope before asking for quotes, [how to write a software RFP](/en/blog/writing-rfp-software).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an app for the Saudi or Qatari market cost less if the team is outside the country?

The tier does not change: a mid-complexity app is $15,000–$45,000 on our rate card, which is SAR 56,500–169,000 or QAR 54,500–164,000. What varies between teams is the hourly rate, $15–$40 for a freelancer and $50–$100 for a regional or local agency, and the overhead a local office adds on top.

### Can a development team outside Saudi Arabia build an app that complies with the PDPL?

Yes. Compliance depends on design decisions (consent, access controls, where data is hosted and whether it leaves the Kingdom under Article 29), not on the team's address. Article 8 makes you responsible for choosing a processor with adequate safeguards, so put the team's data-handling obligations in the contract.

### My company is licensed in DIFC or ADGM. Does the federal UAE data law apply to my app?

The two financial free zones run their own regimes: the DIFC Data Protection Law (DIFC Law No. 5 of 2020) and the ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021. Confirm with your legal adviser which regime covers your licence before the data model is designed, because consent and transfer rules differ.

### When does UAE e-invoicing affect a platform we are building now?

If it issues B2B or B2G invoices. Businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more must be live on 1 January 2027, businesses below that on 1 July 2027, and in-scope government entities on 1 October 2027. The invoicing module then has to connect through an accredited service provider.

### Which payment methods should a Qatar app support beyond cards?

Fawran, the Qatar Central Bank's instant-transfer service launched in March 2024, if you collect payments by bank transfer. It works around the clock, accepts a mobile number or alias instead of an IBAN, and caps each transfer at QR 50,000.

### How much working-day overlap is there with a team based in Syria?

All of it for Riyadh, Jeddah and Doha: Syria, Saudi Arabia and Qatar all keep UTC+3 year-round. Dubai and Abu Dhabi are one hour ahead at UTC+4, so a 9 am start in the UAE is 8 am for the team.
