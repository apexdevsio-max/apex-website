# What AI Actually Costs to Run in Production

**A customer-support assistant handling 3,000 conversations a month costs roughly $36 to $216 a month in model fees at November 2026 list prices.** The top of that range is a mid-tier model with no optimisation; the bottom is the same workload with prompt caching and most calls routed to a small model. Both figures are worked out line by line below from the providers' own pricing pages, read on 17 November 2026.

That bill is separate from building the feature. Adding AI to an existing product is $10,000–$40,000+ on our rate card as of November 2026 (SAR 37,500–150,000+ / AED 36,500–147,000+ / QAR 36,500–145,500+). The model bill then arrives every month for as long as the feature runs, which is what most budgets miss.

---

## How pricing works

**You pay per token, in both directions.** A token is a fragment of text, often part of a word. What you send (input) and what the model returns (output) are both billed, and output costs five times as much as input on every model in the example below.

**Input is larger than people expect.** On every call you are billed for:

- The system prompt and tool definitions
- The entire conversation history you resend
- Any retrieved documents or context
- The user's actual message

**This is why conversations get expensive as they go on.** Turn one sends a short message. Turn ten resends the nine turns before it, so later messages cost several times the first, and nothing in your code looks different.

## Worked example: a support assistant, priced on 17 November 2026

**The workload (assumptions; replace with your own measurements):**

- 3,000 conversations a month, 6 model calls each: **18,000 calls**
- Fixed prefix per call (system prompt, instructions, tool definitions): **2,000 tokens**
- Variable input per call (history, retrieved passages, user message): **2,500 tokens on average**
- Output per call: **300 tokens**

Monthly volume: 36 million prefix tokens, 45 million variable input tokens and 5.4 million output tokens.

**List prices per million tokens, standard tier, read 17 November 2026:**

| Model | Input | Cached input | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Claude Sonnet 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) (Anthropic) | $2.00 | $0.10 | $10.00 |
| [gpt-6.2-sol](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing) (OpenAI) | $2.00 | $0.10 | $10.00 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5, prompts up to 100,000 tokens (Anthropic) | $0.10 | $0.01 | $0.50 |
| gpt-6-luna (OpenAI) | $0.10 | $0.01 | $0.50 |

At these prices the Anthropic and OpenAI models in each pair cost the same, so the arithmetic below holds for either provider.

**The monthly bill:**

| Setup | Calculation | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| Mid-tier model, no caching | 81M × $2 + 5.4M × $10 | **$216.00** |
| Mid-tier model, prefix cached | 36M × $0.10 + 45M × $2 + 5.4M × $10 | **$147.60** |
| Mid-tier model, cached, history trimmed to 1,250 tokens | 36M × $0.10 + 22.5M × $2 + 5.4M × $10 | **$102.60** |
| Small model, prefix cached | 36M × $0.01 + 45M × $0.10 + 5.4M × $0.50 | **$7.56** |
| 80% small, 20% mid, both cached | 0.8 × $7.56 + 0.2 × $147.60 | **$35.57** |

Providers bill in US dollars; at the fixed pegs, $100 is SAR 375, AED 367 or QAR 364.

**What the table shows:**

- **Output is not where this bill goes.** At 300 tokens per reply, output is $54 of the $216. Input, which is resent on every call, is the rest. Trimming what you send moves the bill more than capping replies.
- **Caching alone saved 32% here,** because the cached prefix is billed at 5% of the input price on these two mid-tier models. Anthropic also charges for writing the cache, at $2.50 per million tokens for the 5-minute cache on Sonnet 5.5; at this volume that is a few dollars a month.
- **Routing saved the most.** Sending the simple 80% of questions to a small model cut the bill by 84% against the unoptimised baseline, provided the small model answers those questions well enough. Test that on your own transcripts before you rely on it.
- **Prices move, in both directions.** Google's [Gemini API pricing page](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing) lists gemini-3.8-flash at $0.75 input and $3.75 output per million tokens through 31 December 2026, rising to $1.50 and $7.50 from 1 January 2027. The same workload without caching would go from $81 to $162 a month on the change date. Re-read your provider's pricing page whenever you budget.

## Why Arabic changes the arithmetic

**Token counts depend on the language and on the tokenizer.** Tokenizers are built from training text, and the same meaning can split into a different number of tokens in Arabic than in English. Token counts are not even stable between model generations from one provider: Anthropic's [token counting documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/token-counting) says its newer tokenizer produces approximately 30 percent more tokens for the same input text than earlier models.

**Three consequences:**

1. **An estimate built on English samples does not tell you the Arabic bill.** Measure Arabic directly. If your Arabic transcripts turned out to use 1.5 times the tokens of the English ones (an assumption for illustration, not a measured ratio), the cached mid-tier bill above would rise from $147.60 to $221.40.
2. **Context limits arrive sooner** when the same conversation takes more tokens.
3. **Bilingual products can have uneven costs** per user, depending on the language each user writes in.

**What to do:** count tokens with real Arabic text from your own domain, using the model you will deploy. Anthropic's token counting endpoint is free to use, subject to rate limits. [Choosing an AI model for Arabic](/en/blog/choosing-ai-model-arabic) covers quality as well as cost.

## The three levers, in order of effect

### 1. Route by task difficulty

Teams pick one capable model and send everything through it. Classification, routing, extraction and short answers from a known document rarely need a frontier model; complex reasoning and careful writing do. Handle requests with a small model and escalate to a larger one when the task is complex or confidence is low. In the example above this was the biggest single saving.

### 2. Cache the repeated prefix

If every request sends the same system prompt, instructions or reference documents, caching bills that repeated text at a fraction of the input price. To actually get cache hits, per Anthropic's [prompt caching documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching):

- **Put stable content first.** The cache matches on the prefix in the order tools, system, messages; a change at one level invalidates everything after it.
- **Keep it byte-identical.** Cache hits need 100 percent identical prompt segments, so a timestamp or request ID injected into the system prompt silently disables caching.
- **Mind the lifetime.** The default cache lives 5 minutes and is refreshed each time it is used; low-traffic features may need the paid 1-hour cache or will mostly miss.
- **Mind the minimum.** Prompts shorter than the model's minimum cacheable length (512 tokens on the current Claude models) are processed without caching and no error is returned.
- **Verify.** The API reports cached tokens on each response. Zero cache reads on repeated calls means something in your prefix is changing.

### 3. Send less text

- **Trim conversation history.** Keep recent turns and a summary of earlier ones. Halving the variable input in the example cut $45 a month.
- **Right-size retrieved context.** Retrieved passages are billed as input on every call; see [RAG](/en/blog/rag-knowledge-base) on chunk sizing.
- **Cap output length.** Set a sensible maximum so an occasional runaway answer cannot inflate the bill.
- **Tighten the system prompt.** It is billed, cached or not, on every call for the life of the product.

For work that does not need an immediate answer, such as overnight document processing, Anthropic's Batch API bills input and output at 50 percent of the standard price.

## Estimating before you build

1. **Take a real sample.** Twenty actual interactions from your business: real customer questions, real documents, in the languages your users write.
2. **Count tokens per interaction** with the provider's token counting endpoint, including the system prompt, context and a realistic reply length.
3. **Multiply by realistic volume** for month one, month six and month twelve.
4. **Price it with today's list prices,** as in the worked example, and note the date you read them.
5. **Add a contingency.** We plan with 50% for retries, longer-than-expected inputs and growth.
6. **Compare against what it replaces.** If the assistant saves staff time, the comparison is with that cost, not with zero.

## Controls to build in from the start

- **Spend limits and alerts at the provider,** configured on day one.
- **Per-user rate limits,** so one automated client cannot consume a month's budget in an afternoon.
- **Token usage logged per request,** alongside the user and feature, so you can tell which feature drives cost.
- **Anomaly alerts.** A sudden spike usually means a bug, such as a retry loop or runaway context growth.
- **Cost per outcome.** "Cost per resolved conversation" is a number the business can judge; "monthly API spend" is not.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does an AI assistant with 3,000 conversations a month cost to run?

About $216 a month on a mid-tier model at November 2026 list prices with no optimisation, about $148 with prompt caching, and about $36 if 80% of calls go to a small model. That assumes 6 calls per conversation and 4,500 input and 300 output tokens per call.

### Why is input, not output, most of my bill when output costs more per token?

Because input is resent on every call: the system prompt, the whole conversation so far and any retrieved passages. In the worked example input was $162 of a $216 bill even though output costs five times as much per token.

### How much does prompt caching actually save?

In the example, 32%: the 2,000-token fixed prefix drops from $2 to $0.10 per million tokens on a mid-tier model. The saving grows with the share of each request that is identical, and disappears if a timestamp or request ID changes the prefix.

### Will my Arabic users cost more to serve than English users?

They can, because the same meaning may take a different number of tokens in Arabic. Do not guess a ratio: count tokens on real Arabic transcripts with the model you will deploy, then price the result.

### How often should I re-check model prices?

Every time you budget. In the month this article was updated, Google listed a price for gemini-3.8-flash that doubles on 1 January 2027, while Anthropic and OpenAI priced their mid-tier models at $2 and $10 per million tokens.

### Is the running cost included in the build price?

No. Building an AI feature into an existing product is $10,000–$40,000+ on our rate card as of November 2026; model fees, hosting and vector search are billed monthly by the providers you use.
