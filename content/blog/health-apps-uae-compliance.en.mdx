# Health Apps in the UAE: Dubai Health Authority Requirements

**To run a telehealth app in Dubai you need a Dubai Health Authority (DHA) licensed health facility, a DHA-approved platform that stores its data in the UAE with a DESC-certified cloud provider, and ISO 27001 and HIPAA compliance certification for that platform.** Those rules come from the [DHA Standards for Telehealth Services, version 4](https://dha.gov.ae/uploads/012023/Standards%20for%20Telehealth%20Services2023158613.pdf), issued on 26 September 2025 and in effect since 26 November 2025. Federal health-data law applies on top, and Abu Dhabi has its own regulator.

> Written from an engineering perspective, not legal advice. Current as of November 2026.

This article covers Dubai in detail because DHA's standard is the one we could read in full. The federal and Abu Dhabi sections are shorter and say plainly where you need to check with the regulator. For how health licensing sits on top of data protection law, see the [Gulf regulatory compliance guide](/en/blog/gulf-compliance-guide). ## 1. Licensing comes before code (Dubai)

The DHA standard is explicit that the platform alone is not enough:

**A licensed facility.** "The provision of telehealth services shall only be offered through a DHA licensed telehealth facility" (section 7.1). DHA's licence categories are a standalone telehealth centre, telehealth added to an existing facility, an added booth, and telehealth platforms (section 7.2.1).

**A platform licence is not a clinical licence.** A telehealth platform's role is limited to a DHA-approved tool that connects licensed facilities to patients. Platforms may not deliver clinical services, may not operate as a healthcare provider, and may not recruit clinicians to deliver care through the platform (sections 7.8–7.8.3).

**Platform approval.** Every telehealth platform must be assessed and approved by DHA before in-house or go-to-market use. It also needs legal representation in Dubai with a trade licence and an assigned Business Technical Director (sections 7.6.2–7.6.4).

**Licensed clinicians and a medical director.** Physicians must be DHA-licensed, including those residing outside the UAE who consult patients in the UAE (section 6.2). The standard defines the medical director as a DHA-licensed physician or dentist with clinical oversight of the facility.

**Accreditation.** Telehealth providers must obtain telehealth accreditation within 24 months of initial licensure or licence renewal (section 5.2.2). The standard names QAI, EIAC, JCI, URAC and NCQA as examples of accrediting bodies (section 5.3).

**What this means for a technology company:** you either partner with a DHA-licensed facility that carries the clinical licence, or you obtain one yourself. Start that track during design, not after the build.

## 2. Data residency is an architectural constraint

The DHA standard puts it in one line: "The storage, development, or transfer health data and health-related information outside the UAE is prohibited unless approved by DHA" (section 8.2.5(d)).

For platforms it adds concrete hosting rules (section 7.6.7):

- Data storage must comply with Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the use of ICT in health fields.
- All data centres must be at least Tier 3 certified.
- All data must be stored on a server at a cloud service provider certified by the Dubai Electronic Security Centre (DESC) in the UAE.
- Health data related to services provided in the UAE may not be stored, developed or transferred outside the country, except in the cases in Article 2 of Ministerial Decision No. 51 of 2021.

**What to verify technically:**

| Item | What to check |
|---|---|
| Hosting | A DESC-certified provider and a UAE region, Tier 3 or above |
| Backups | Not replicated to a region outside the UAE |
| Logs | Your logging or error-tracking service does not ship records abroad |
| Analytics | The tool processes data in the UAE, or receives no health data |
| Notifications and email | External providers do not carry clinical content |
| AI | Sending patient data to a model hosted abroad is a transfer |

> **The mistake to avoid:** checking where the primary database lives and stopping there. Backups, logs and failover copies are data too. The [data residency guide](/en/blog/data-residency-gulf) walks through the places residency usually breaks.

## 3. Integration with NABIDH

Dubai's health information exchange is NABIDH, which the [UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/whole-of-government-approach/digital-health/Digital-health-ecosystem) describes as developed by DHA. The telehealth standard requires the IT infrastructure to integrate with the provider's electronic health record system, and the health records system "shall integrate with Nabidh" (section 7.9.1(e)).

**For the build, that means:**
- Model your clinical data so it can be exchanged with NABIDH from the first version.
- Budget time for the integration and its approval as its own work item.
- Restructuring a data model after launch costs far more than designing for the exchange up front.

## 4. Security and certification requirements

**ISO 27001 and HIPAA are required for platforms.** The DHA standard states that "all platforms must have ISO 27001 compliance certification" and HIPAA compliance certification, and that some applicants may have to meet a higher bar such as HITRUST or SOC 2 with HIPAA alignment (section 7.6.7(c)–(e)). Certification takes months, so start it in parallel with development.

**Encrypted platforms.** Physicians must use DHA-approved encrypted platforms (section 6.5.2), and patient consent must cover the security measures used, such as encryption, password protection and user authentication (section 10.2.3).

**Approved communication channels.** Platforms must ensure all communication channels are approved by the TDRA (section 7.6.6).

**Language.** The standard requires information for the patient in a language they can understand (section 9.6.3). In practice that means Arabic and English with correct right-to-left layout; see the [Flutter RTL guide](/en/blog/flutter-rtl-arabic-guide).

**AI features.** Providers using AI, including ambient AI, are subject to specific requirements in line with DHA's AI policy (section 5.4).

## 5. Federal rules: Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 and MOHAP

Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the use of ICT in health fields regulates ICT in the UAE healthcare sector "including its free zones", according to the [UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/whole-of-government-approach/digital-health/ict-in-the-health-sector). It also lets the Ministry of Health and Prevention (MOHAP) collect, analyse and keep health information at national level. The in-country rule for health data that DHA applies comes from this law and Ministerial Decision No. 51 of 2021.

The DHA standard also names several MOHAP roles that matter to a product team:

- MOHAP approval for importing and using medical devices, for example in a telehealth booth (section 7.5.5(b)).
- Equipment and technology for telehealth delivery approved by MOHAP (section 5.5.4).
- Advertising for telehealth services must meet MOHAP's medical advertisement requirements (section 7.3).

The [UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws) lists this health law separately from the federal Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021), and the DHA standard refers to both (section 11.1.1). **Questions for your lawyer:** how the two laws apply together to your data, and which of the Ministerial Decision 51 exceptions, if any, covers a transfer you plan.

## 6. Abu Dhabi: a different regulator and a different exchange

Abu Dhabi's health regulator is the Department of Health – Abu Dhabi (DoH), and its health information exchange is [Malaffi](https://malaffi.ae/about/), which connects public and private providers in the emirate. The federal health-data law above applies in Abu Dhabi as well.

We could not load DoH's own standards pages, so this article does not describe DoH's licensing or technical rules. **Put these to the regulator or your advisor before you build for Abu Dhabi:**

- Which DoH facility or telemedicine licence covers your service model?
- Does DoH's healthcare information and cyber security standard (ADHICS) apply to you, and to your hosting provider?
- When and how must your system connect to Malaffi?
- Does a DHA-approved platform need a separate approval to serve patients in Abu Dhabi?

DHA's own standard says providers giving continuity of care to patients outside Dubai must meet the regulatory requirements of that jurisdiction (section 5.1.2). One build can serve both emirates, but expect two approval tracks.

## 7. What makes your app regulated?

Do not assume a "light" health app is out of scope. DHA's standard lists **scheduling appointments** among telehealth services in its introduction, alongside assessment, medical advice, treatment, monitoring and prescribing. Whether your product counts as a telehealth service is a classification question to settle with DHA, not an engineering judgement.

| Feature | Question to ask DHA |
|---|---|
| Appointment booking only | Is this a telehealth service in our model, or a facility's admin tool? |
| Medication reminders | Does the level of guidance make it clinical? |
| Electronic medical records | Which storage, NABIDH and access rules apply? |
| Video consultation | Teleconsultation: full facility, platform and clinician licensing |
| Diagnosis or decision support | Telediagnostics or AI rules on top of the above |

**The practical recommendation:** keep the first version narrow and get the classification in writing. Our [clinic booking app cost guide](/en/blog/clinic-booking-app-cost) prices the product side.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a technology company run a telehealth platform in Dubai without a clinical licence?

It can hold a telehealth platform licence, but that licence "does not grant the authority to operate as a healthcare provider", and platforms may not deliver clinical services or recruit clinicians (DHA standard, section 7.8). Care must come from a DHA-licensed facility, so the usual model is a platform partnered with a licensed facility.

### Can a DHA telehealth platform run on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud?

Only if the setup meets the DHA rules: data stored on a server at a cloud provider certified by DESC in the UAE, in a Tier 3 or higher data centre, with no health data leaving the UAE outside the Ministerial Decision 51 of 2021 exceptions. Check the provider's DESC certification and turn off any cross-region backup or log shipping.

### Is ISO 27001 really mandatory for a Dubai telehealth platform?

Yes. Section 7.6.7(d) of the DHA Standards for Telehealth Services says all platforms must have ISO 27001 compliance certification, and section 7.6.7(c) requires HIPAA compliance certification. DHA may ask some applicants for HITRUST or SOC 2 as well.

### Does an appointment-booking app fall under the DHA telehealth standard?

Possibly. The standard's introduction lists scheduling appointments among telehealth services. Ask DHA how it classifies your model before you build, and get the answer in writing.

### Does DHA approval let me serve patients in Abu Dhabi?

Not by itself. DHA's standard says care for patients outside Dubai must meet that jurisdiction's requirements (section 5.1.2). Abu Dhabi is regulated by the Department of Health – Abu Dhabi and uses the Malaffi exchange, so check DoH's licensing and integration rules separately.

### What changed when version 4 of the DHA telehealth standard took effect?

Version 4 took effect on 26 November 2025 and is due for revision on 26 September 2030. It covers licence categories for platforms and booths, hosting and certification rules for platforms, accreditation within 24 months, telehealth across borders, and specific rules for AI including ambient AI. If you built against an earlier version, compare your platform with sections 5 to 7.
