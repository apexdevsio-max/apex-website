# ZATCA E-Invoicing for Online Stores: What Fatoora Actually Requires

If your Saudi store's VAT-subject revenue exceeded **SAR 187,500** in any year from 2022 to 2025, ZATCA's Phase Two rules reach you by **1 February 2027** at the latest: that is the Wave 25 integration date ZATCA [announced on 24 July 2026](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/Wave25-E-invoicing.aspx). From then on, every simplified (consumer) invoice your store issues must carry a cryptographic stamp and be **reported to ZATCA's Fatoora platform within 24 hours**, and every standard (business) invoice must be **cleared by ZATCA before the buyer receives it**.

Most store owners meet this without understanding it, because their platform handles it. That works until you move to a custom store, add a second sales channel, or an auditor asks a question nobody can answer. This guide sets out the dated facts, what implementing them involves, and where the work sits.

> Written from an engineering perspective, not legal advice. Current as of November 2026.

Tax rules change and ZATCA announces new waves on a rolling basis, so check your own notification and confirm your obligations with a qualified Saudi tax advisor.

---

## The two phases, with dates

**Phase One (Generation)** has applied since **4 December 2021**. Businesses had to stop issuing handwritten or free-form invoices and produce structured electronic ones with mandatory fields, plus a QR code on simplified invoices. This comes from ZATCA's [E-Invoicing Implementation Resolution](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/Introduction/LawsAndRegulations/Documents/E-Invoicing%20Implementation%20Resolution_EN.pdf). ZATCA's English text notes that the Arabic version is the official one.

**Phase Two (Integration)** has applied in waves since **1 January 2023**. Invoices must be produced in ZATCA's XML format, carry a cryptographic stamp, and be exchanged with the Fatoora platform through ZATCA's APIs. The same resolution sets two different models:

| Invoice type | Typical buyer | Model | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard tax invoice (and its notes) | Another business (B2B) | **Clearance**: ZATCA validates the invoice and applies its stamp | Before the invoice is shared with the buyer |
| Simplified tax invoice (and its notes) | A consumer (B2C) | **Reporting**: you stamp and issue, then send to ZATCA | Within **24 hours** of generation |

A store that sells to consumers mostly issues simplified invoices, so the 24-hour reporting window is the one that shapes your system design.

---

## The integration waves

ZATCA picks each wave by VAT-subject revenue and notifies the taxpayers it targets **at least six months before** their integration date. These are the waves at either end of the rollout, from ZATCA's own announcements:

| Wave | Revenue threshold (VAT-subject) | Revenue years counted | Integration date | Announced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Above SAR 3 billion | 2021 | From 1 January 2023 | [24 June 2022](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/E-Invoicing-Phase-2-ntegration.aspx) |
| 24 | Above SAR 375,000 | 2022, 2023 or 2024 | By 30 June 2026 | [26 September 2025](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/Pages/news-1426.aspx) |
| 25 | Above SAR 187,500 | 2022, 2023, 2024 or 2025 | 1 February 2027 | [24 July 2026](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/Wave25-E-invoicing.aspx) |

Waves 2 to 23 fall between these points; your own ZATCA notification gives the date that binds you. If your revenue was above SAR 375,000 in 2022–2024, your integration date has already passed. If it only crossed SAR 187,500, start now: certificate onboarding and testing against Fatoora have to be finished before February 2027, not started then.

## What this means for a store, practically

Your store needs to:

1. **Generate a compliant invoice** for every sale, in ZATCA's XML structure with all mandatory fields
2. **Apply a cryptographic stamp** using a certificate issued through ZATCA's onboarding
3. **Produce a QR code** encoding the required invoice data
4. **Exchange with Fatoora**: report simplified invoices within 24 hours, clear standard invoices before sending them
5. **Keep a tamper-resistant invoice counter** that cannot be reset, as the resolution requires
6. **Store invoices** under the VAT record-keeping rules
7. **Handle failures**: what happens when the API call fails and the customer is waiting

Item seven separates a working implementation from a demo. Network calls fail and external platforms have maintenance windows. Checkout cannot hang because Fatoora is slow, and it cannot skip the invoice either. Because simplified invoices are reported *after* issue, you can issue the invoice at once and report it from a queue that retries until it succeeds, as long as it does so inside the 24-hour window. Standard invoices are harder, because clearance has to finish before the invoice is valid to share.

This is integration work on the store's server side, the same kind of build our [web development team](/en/services/web-development) does for custom stores.

## Solution providers: what ZATCA's list does and does not mean

ZATCA publishes a [Solution Providers Directory](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/SolutionProviders/Pages/SolutionProvidersDirectory.aspx) of vendors that passed its qualification process. ZATCA states plainly that the list is a non-binding guide, that it **is not an approval** of those solutions, and that a taxpayer whose solution meets the requirements is compliant even if the vendor is not listed.

For a custom store, the choice is between:

- **Integrating directly** with ZATCA's APIs: more control and more work, and you own correctness and every specification update
- **Using a solution provider's API**: less build work, a recurring fee, and someone else tracks specification changes

For most stores the second is the better trade, because the specification keeps changing and keeping up with it never ends.

## Questions to ask any vendor

If someone is quoting you for e-invoicing, these five answers show whether they have done it before:

1. **Are you integrating directly with ZATCA or through a solution provider?** Both are valid; the answer should be deliberate.
2. **How do you meet the 24-hour reporting window when Fatoora is unreachable?** Listen for queue, retry and alerting, not "it just sends".
3. **Who holds the cryptographic certificate, and how is it renewed?** An expired certificate stops stamping, so it stops invoicing.
4. **How are refunds and credit notes handled?** The resolution applies the same clearance and reporting rules to the notes linked to each invoice.
5. **What happens when the specification changes?** Is that covered by maintenance, or billed as new work each time?

The fourth question catches more incomplete implementations than any other.

## Common mistakes

**Assuming the platform covers everything.** Hosted platforms handle the standard checkout. If you also sell through a mobile app, a marketplace, or in person at an event, confirm those are covered too.

**Forgetting credit notes.** Refunds and cancellations need their own compliant electronic notes. A store that issues invoices correctly and handles refunds informally is only half compliant.

**Not monitoring the reporting queue.** A queue that silently stops draining becomes a breach of the 24-hour window. Alert on queue age, not just on errors.

**Treating it as a launch task.** Certificates renew, specifications change, and new waves arrive. ZATCA's [published e-invoicing violations](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/E-invoicing-Violations-Fines.aspx) from November 2021 already set fines of SAR 5,000 for not issuing e-invoices and SAR 10,000 for deleting or amending them after issue. Budget it as maintenance, see [In-House Maintenance or a Contract?](/en/blog/maintenance-inhouse-vs-contract).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Phase Two wave is my store in?

ZATCA selects waves by VAT-subject revenue and notifies the taxpayers it targets at least six months ahead. If your revenue exceeded SAR 375,000 in 2022, 2023 or 2024, Wave 24's deadline of 30 June 2026 has passed. If it exceeded SAR 187,500 in any year from 2022 to 2025, Wave 25's integration date is 1 February 2027. Check your own ZATCA notification for the date that binds you.

### How long do I have to report a simplified invoice to ZATCA?

No more than 24 hours from when the invoice is generated, under ZATCA's E-Invoicing Implementation Resolution. Standard (B2B) invoices work differently: ZATCA must clear them before you share them with the buyer.

### Is a vendor on ZATCA's solution provider list certified?

No. ZATCA describes the directory as a non-binding guide, says listing is not an approval of the solution, and accepts any solution that meets the requirements, listed or not.

### What does e-invoicing add to the cost of a custom Saudi store?

There is no separate rate-card line. It is part of the build: an e-commerce app is $20,000 – $65,000 (SAR 75,000 – 243,800) and a custom web application is $25,000 – $80,000 (SAR 93,800 – 300,000), from our published rate card as of November 2026. Using a solution provider's API reduces build work in exchange for a recurring fee.

### Do refunds need e-invoices too?

Yes. Credit and debit notes follow the same clearance or reporting model as the invoice they relate to, and they are the part most often missed in a first implementation.

### Does a Saudi implementation cover the UAE?

No. The UAE runs a separate programme under its Ministry of Finance, built around accredited service providers. In [May 2026](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-targeted-amendments-to-einvoicing-system-decisions/) the ministry confirmed that businesses with annual revenue above AED 50 million must fully implement it by 1 January 2027. Treat each country as a separate integration.
