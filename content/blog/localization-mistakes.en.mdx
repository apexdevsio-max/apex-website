# 12 Arabic Localisation Mistakes and How to Fix Them

The mistakes that make an Arabic product feel translated rather than native are, in short: letter-spacing on Arabic text, a separate RTL stylesheet, mirroring the wrong things, mixed digit systems, fixed-height components, hard-coded strings, two plural forms instead of six, concatenated sentences, broken bidirectional punctuation, unreviewed machine translation, English backend messages, and translated keyword lists. Each one below comes with its symptom, its cause and its fix.

Most are individually small; together they are the difference between an app Arabic users trust and one they describe as "translated". For the underlying method, see the [complete Arabic localisation guide](/en/blog/arabic-localization-guide). If you are building rather than fixing, we avoid this list by design in our [mobile app](/en/services/mobile-apps) and [web development](/en/services/web-development) work.

---

## 1. Letter-spacing applied to Arabic

**Symptom:** Arabic text looks broken — letters appear disconnected, like a word cut into pieces.

**Cause:** a `letter-spacing` value inherited from a design system built for Latin type.

**Why it matters more than it sounds:** Arabic letters join and change shape by position. MDN's [`letter-spacing` reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/letter-spacing) notes that Arabic-script text expects connected letters to stay connected and that letter spacing "may lead to the text looking broken". Every native reader notices it instantly.

**Fix:** `letter-spacing: 0` for all Arabic text (`letterSpacing: 0` in React Native). Audit your design tokens, because this usually comes from a global heading style rather than a deliberate decision.

---

## 2. A separate RTL stylesheet

**Symptom:** RTL layout is correct for older components and broken for anything added recently.

**Cause:** `styles-rtl.css` overriding physical properties. Every new component needs two implementations, and only one gets tested.

**Fix:** [logical CSS properties](/en/blog/rtl-css-guide) — `margin-inline-start` instead of `margin-left` — so one stylesheet is correct in both directions. Add a lint rule rejecting physical properties in new code and convert existing components as you touch them.

---

## 3. Mirroring the wrong things

**Symptom:** the play button points left, the clock looks reversed, phone numbers show the country code at the wrong end — or, in Flutter, back arrows point left in Arabic.

**Cause:** a blanket `transform: scaleX(-1)` applied to all icons under RTL; or, in Flutter, wrapping Material icons in `Transform.flip` even though icons such as `Icons.arrow_back` are defined with [`matchTextDirection: true`](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/widgets/IconData/matchTextDirection.html) and already mirror themselves.

**Fix:** flip only directional icons that do not already mirror — custom back arrows, next/previous chevrons, send icons. Media controls, clocks, time-axis charts and anything where character order carries meaning stay as they are. [Arabic and RTL in Flutter](/en/blog/flutter-rtl-arabic-guide) shows the correct pattern.

## 4. Mixed numeral systems

**Symptom:** a price in `١٢٣` beside an order number in `123`.

**Cause:** no single decision about digits, so different developers — or different locale defaults — made different choices. In current ICU data, for example, `ar-SA` defaults to Eastern Arabic digits while `ar-AE` defaults to Western ones.

**Fix:** choose one system, set it explicitly (`ar-SA-u-nu-latn` or `-u-nu-arab` with `Intl.NumberFormat`), and never hard-code a digit — see [Arabic numerals and formats](/en/blog/arabic-numerals-formats).

---

## 5. Fixed-height components

**Symptom:** Arabic text clipped in buttons and cards, or overflowing its container.

**Cause:** components sized around English text. Arabic strings differ in length and render taller, and Arabic needs more line height.

**Fix:** `min-height` instead of `height`, and test every component with real Arabic content — not with Latin placeholder text, which hides the problem entirely.

---

## 6. Hard-coded strings found during QA

**Symptom:** English text appearing in an otherwise Arabic interface, discovered late and scattered everywhere.

**Cause:** no i18n layer at the start, so strings went straight into components.

**Fix:** build the [i18n layer](/en/blog/i18n-arabic-guide) in phase one. Check where strings hide: validation messages, email templates, error handlers, chart labels, PDF generators and push notification text.

---

## 7. Two plural forms instead of six

**Symptom:** Arabic quantities read as grammatically wrong to native speakers, especially for two items.

**Cause:** an i18n setup that only handles singular and plural, because that is what English needs. The [CLDR plural rules](https://www.unicode.org/cldr/charts/latest/supplemental/language_plural_rules.html) give Arabic six categories: zero, one, two, few (3–10), many (11–99) and other.

**Fix:** use a library built on `Intl.PluralRules` or ICU MessageFormat and write all six forms. The dual for exactly two is the one most noticeably missing.

---

## 8. Concatenated sentence fragments

**Symptom:** Arabic sentences with words in the wrong order.

**Cause:** building sentences from pieces — `t("youHave") + count + t("items")` — which assumes English word order.

**Fix:** one string per sentence with named placeholders. Never assemble sentences from translated fragments.

---

## 9. Broken bidirectional punctuation

**Symptom:** a full stop on the wrong side of a sentence, a `#` detached from its order code, or a currency symbol on the wrong side of a price.

**Cause:** a missing or wrong base direction, or Latin text and numbers embedded in Arabic without isolation, so neutral characters resolve against the surrounding text.

**Fix:** `dir="rtl"` on Arabic containers; `<bdi>` around embedded or user-generated text, as the W3C's [inline bidi guidance](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/) recommends; `dir="ltr"` on phone numbers and IBANs; the U+2066 … U+2069 isolate characters in mobile and plain-text strings; and `Intl.NumberFormat` for currency rather than concatenating a symbol and a number.

---

## 10. Machine translation shipped without review

**Symptom:** text that is technically correct and reads as machine-produced. Native users describe the product as unserious.

**Cause:** treating translation as a mechanical step rather than a content step.

**Fix:** machine translation can be a first draft for volume content. Always have a fluent reader review before release; it cannot make register decisions or adapt examples, and that is where perceived quality lives.

---

## 11. Backend surfaces left in English

**Symptom:** a fully Arabic app that sends English confirmation emails and English PDF invoices.

**Cause:** localisation scoped to the interface only.

**Fix:** include emails, SMS, PDF documents and API error messages in the translation scope from the start. They are among the most commonly overlooked surfaces.

---

## 12. Arabic SEO as translated English keywords

**Symptom:** Arabic content that ranks for nothing.

**Cause:** translating the English keyword list literally. Arabic speakers phrase queries differently and often mix Arabic and English.

**Fix:** do Arabic keyword research independently, as an original exercise rather than a translation — see [SEO vs GEO](/en/blog/seo-vs-geo). The same applies inside your product: [Arabic search](/en/blog/arabic-search-guide) explains why exact matching fails.

## The pattern behind all twelve

Most of these are cheap to prevent and expensive to fix, for the same reason: **they are decisions baked into the first weeks of a project.** Design tokens, style conventions, the i18n layer and translation scope are set early and touched by everything afterwards.

**The three habits that prevent most of this list:**

1. **Build the i18n layer in phase one**, whatever your launch language.
2. **Use logical properties or directional widgets from the first component.** The W3C's [guidance on RTL markup](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-html-dir) recommends writing styles with start and end rather than left and right for exactly this reason.
3. **Test in RTL in week one**, with real Arabic text, not placeholder Latin.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do the letters in my Arabic heading look disconnected?

Letter-spacing. Arabic letters join, and any non-zero letter-spacing separates them; MDN warns it may make Arabic text look broken. Set `letter-spacing: 0` for Arabic, and check your design tokens — it usually comes from a global heading style.

### Why do back arrows point the wrong way in my Flutter app?

Probably because they are flipped twice. Material icons such as `Icons.arrow_back` are defined with `matchTextDirection: true`, so `Icon` already mirrors them in RTL; an extra `Transform.flip` reverses them again. Remove the wrapper and keep manual flipping for your own SVGs and images.

### Why is my Arabic text getting clipped in buttons?

The component has a fixed height sized around English text. Arabic renders taller and needs more line height, so it overflows or clips. Use `min-height` instead of `height` and test with real Arabic content.

### Why does Arabic need six plural forms when English needs two?

Arabic grammar distinguishes zero, one, two, few, many and other, including a dual for exactly two. The CLDR rules map numbers to each category, for example `few` for 3–10 and `many` for 11–99. An i18n setup built for English only knows singular and plural, so most Arabic quantities come out wrong.

### Which of these mistakes are expensive to fix after launch?

The architectural ones: a missing i18n layer, physical style properties, and fixed-height components, because every part of the codebase touches them. Letter-spacing and icon mirroring are quick fixes by comparison.

### Why does my Arabic content not rank in search?

Usually because the keywords were translated from an English list rather than researched in Arabic. Arabic speakers phrase queries differently and often mix Arabic and English, so a literal translation targets queries nobody types.

### My Arabic app already has these problems. Where do I start?

With the typography: letter-spacing and line height are quick and give the most visible improvement. Then add a lint rule that blocks new physical CSS properties, so the problem stops growing while you convert existing components.

## Where to begin

Fix what readers see first — letter-spacing, line height, icon direction, digits — then stop the architectural mistakes from spreading with an i18n layer and logical properties. Nearly every item above is obvious at a glance to someone reading Arabic on a real device, and nearly invisible to a team reviewing Latin placeholder text.
