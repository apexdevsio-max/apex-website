# App Store Rejections: Why Apps Get Rejected and How to Avoid It

**Apple reviews at least half of submissions in under 24 hours and 90% in under 48 hours, according to its [App Review page](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/). Google Play says some reviews take [up to seven days or longer](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859751), and a new personal Play account must first run a 14-day closed test with 12 testers.** The rejections that cost the most time come from a short list: privacy declarations that do not match the app, missing in-app account deletion, payment rules, and reviewers who cannot get past the login screen.

> Written from an engineering perspective, not legal advice. Current as of November 2026.

This guide covers what the store rules actually say, and the regional points that catch Gulf launches. If you are planning a [mobile app](/en/services/mobile-apps), put the timings below into the schedule from day one.

---

## Realistic timelines

| Stage | What the store says |
|---|---|
| Apple review | At least 50% of submissions in under 24 hours, 90% in under 48 hours ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)) |
| Google Play review | Some apps get extended review of up to 7 days, longer in exceptional cases ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859751)) |
| New Google Play personal account | Closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 consecutive days before production ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)) |

**How we plan it:** for Apple, allow a week for a first submission including one rejection and fix. For Google, if the app is published from a personal account created after 13 November 2023, the closed test alone is at least 14 days — so recruit the 12 testers while the app is still in development. Testers who leave and rejoin restart their 14-day count. Google states this rule for personal accounts; a company launching a product should normally publish from an organisation account anyway, so set that up early.

Apple also allows an **expedited review** request for a critical bug fix or an app tied to a dated event you are directly associated with.

## The rejections that actually happen

### 1. Privacy declarations that do not match the app

Apple's guideline 2.3 asks that all metadata, "including privacy information", accurately reflects the app. Google is blunter: the developer is "alone" responsible for complete and accurate [Data safety](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469) declarations, and these must include data collected "through any third-party libraries or SDKs". Apps that do not correct a discrepancy face blocked updates or removal.

**Why it happens:** an advertising, analytics or attribution SDK collects data the team never audited.

**The fix:** list what every dependency transmits before filling in either form, and make your [privacy policy](/en/blog/privacy-policy-guide) say the same thing. Apple's guideline 5.1.1(i) also requires a privacy policy link both in App Store Connect and inside the app.

### 2. Missing account deletion

- **Apple, guideline 5.1.1(v):** "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app."
- **Google Play:** an in-app path to delete the account and associated data **and** a [web link](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111) where users can request deletion, declared in the Data safety form.

A support email address satisfies neither. Deletion is also more work than it looks: the backend has to remove or anonymise the data, not just hide the account. Saudi Arabia's [PDPL](https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/portal/pdp/knowledgecenter/details/PDPL) gives users the right to ask for destruction of data that is no longer needed (Article 4) and requires destruction once the purpose ends (Article 18). How that applies to backups is a good question for your lawyer; the engineering answer is a documented backup expiry period.

### 3. Payment rules

- **Apple 3.1.1:** unlocking features or content — subscriptions, premium content, a full version — must use in-app purchase.
- **Apple 3.1.3(e):** physical goods or services "consumed outside of the app" must use other payment methods, such as Apple Pay or card entry.
- **Google Play:** [Play Billing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738) is required for in-app digital goods and services, and must not be used for physical goods and services such as transport or food delivery. Google runs alternative-billing programmes in eligible countries; check whether yours is one before designing around them.

**Where teams get caught:** a subscription that unlocks in-app lessons or content, routed through a card gateway. An Arabic education app such as [Tafawwoq](/en/portfolio/tafawwoq-educational-app), which combines video lessons with subscription management, is exactly the kind of product where 3.1.1 has to be designed in from the start. If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, settle it before building the payment flow.

### 4. Reviewers cannot get in

Apple's guideline 2.1(a) asks for demo account details "(and turn on your back-end service!)" if the app has a login; Google's [Prepare your app for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455) page asks for sign-in details for restricted areas. Rejections follow from:

- No demo account for a login-gated app
- Demo credentials that expired or were deleted
- An OTP sent to a local phone number the reviewer cannot receive
- Features that need hardware or a region the reviewer does not have

**The fix:** a working demo account, checked on the day you submit, and written instructions for anything unusual. If legal or security rules prevent a demo account, Apple allows a built-in demo mode with prior approval.

### 5. Broken links and placeholder content

Privacy policy URLs returning 404, support pages that do not exist, "Lorem ipsum" in a settings screen. Trivial to avoid, and covered by Apple's completeness rule (2.1).

### 6. Not enough "app"

Apple's guideline 4.2 says an app should go beyond "a repackaged website". If your app is essentially your site in a shell, read [mobile app or responsive website](/en/blog/app-vs-pwa) — the reviewer may be telling you something true.

### 7. Permissions without a clear reason

Apple 5.1.1(ii) requires purpose strings that "clearly and completely describe your use of the data". Ask for location, contacts, camera or microphone only where the reason is visible in the app's behaviour.

## Gulf launch points

**Local law applies, not just the guidelines.** Apple's section 5 says apps "must comply with all legal requirements in any location where you make them available", and that it is your responsibility to know them. For data protection in Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar, start with the [Gulf compliance guide](/en/blog/gulf-compliance-guide).

**Regulated sectors need the right account.** Apple 5.1.1(ix) says apps in highly regulated fields — banking and financial services, healthcare, gambling, air travel, crypto exchanges — should be submitted by the legal entity that provides the service, not an individual developer. Google Play requires a [Financial features declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821) for any app with financial features and may ask for licence documents. See [fintech in Saudi Arabia](/en/blog/fintech-sama-compliance) and [health apps in the UAE](/en/blog/health-apps-uae-compliance) for the local licences.

**Arabic listing and right-to-left screenshots.** No store rule requires an Arabic listing, but guideline 2.3 does require screenshots that reflect the app's core experience — so if the app is Arabic-first, show it in Arabic. Localise the title, description, keywords and screenshots; see the [Arabic localisation guide](/en/blog/arabic-localization-guide).

**Local payment methods still follow store rules.** A card gateway that supports mada is fine for physical goods and real-world services, not for unlocking digital content — see [Gulf payment gateways](/en/blog/gulf-payment-gateways).

## A pre-submission checklist

**Accounts and access**
- [ ] Working demo account, details in the review notes
- [ ] Credentials re-checked on submission day; OTP bypass or demo mode for reviewers
- [ ] Google Play: organisation account, or the 12-tester, 14-day closed test completed

**Privacy**
- [ ] Every SDK audited for what it transmits
- [ ] Apple privacy details and Google Data safety form match the app
- [ ] Privacy policy URL live, linked in the store listing and inside the app

**Functionality**
- [ ] In-app account deletion that deletes; Google web deletion link live
- [ ] No placeholder content or dead links
- [ ] Every permission has a clear purpose string
- [ ] Payment method correct for what you sell (3.1.1 / 3.1.3(e) / Play Billing)

**Regional**
- [ ] Arabic listing metadata and Arabic screenshots if the app is Arabic-first
- [ ] Submitted by the licensed legal entity for regulated services
- [ ] Financial features declaration completed on Google Play, if relevant

## When you are rejected

**Read the cited guideline.** Fix what it names, not what you assume the reviewer meant.

**You can reply.** Apple lets you correspond with App Review in App Store Connect before resubmitting, and you can appeal to the **App Review Board** if you believe your app was misunderstood — one appeal per rejected submission, with specific reasons. Google Play's [enforcement emails](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234) include instructions on how to appeal.

**Fix and resubmit promptly,** and do not bundle unrelated changes into the fix: new code is new surface for a second rejection.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does Apple App Review take in 2026?

Apple says App Review typically handles at least 50% of submissions in under 24 hours and 90% in under 48 hours. Incomplete submissions take longer or fail. Plan about a week for a first submission so one rejection and fix does not move your launch date.

### What is the 12-tester rule on Google Play?

Personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for 14 consecutive days before applying for production access. A tester who leaves and rejoins restarts the count. Google states the rule for personal accounts, so a company should normally publish from an organisation account.

### Is a support email enough for account deletion?

No. Apple guideline 5.1.1(v) requires account deletion inside the app if the app supports account creation. Google Play requires an in-app deletion path and a web link where users can request deletion of the account and associated data.

### Can a Gulf subscription app take payment through mada or a card gateway?

Only for physical goods or real-world services. Subscriptions or purchases that unlock digital content inside the app must use Apple in-app purchase under guideline 3.1.1 and Google Play Billing, unless you qualify for one of Google's alternative-billing programmes in an eligible country.

### Who should submit a fintech or health app to the App Store?

The licensed legal entity that provides the service. Apple guideline 5.1.1(ix) says apps in highly regulated fields such as banking, financial services and healthcare should not be submitted by an individual developer. On Google Play, apps with financial features must also complete the Financial features declaration.

### Can I appeal an App Store rejection?

Yes. You can reply to App Review in App Store Connect, or file one appeal per rejected submission with the App Review Board, giving specific reasons why the app complies. Google Play includes appeal instructions in its enforcement emails.
