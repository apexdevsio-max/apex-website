# How Much Does an E-Commerce Mobile App Cost?

A custom e-commerce app costs **$20,000 to $65,000** (SAR 75,000–244,000 / AED 73,500–238,500 / QAR 73,000–236,500) and takes **4 to 7 months** to build, as of November 2026. Where you land in that range depends mostly on catalogue complexity, the number of payment methods, and how much of your existing store the app has to stay in sync with.

The less comfortable part of the answer: many store owners who ask for an app do not need one yet. An app is a second storefront with its own build, release process and maintenance bill. It earns its place when you already have customers who come back; it wastes money when you are still trying to find them.

Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. The [mobile app development cost guide](/en/blog/mobile-app-development-cost) explains how every tier is built up; this article covers what moves a store app within its tier.

**About the app shell.** Wrapping your mobile site in an app frame is the cheapest way to get a home-screen icon and push notifications. The catch is Apple: [App Review Guideline 4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) says an app should "elevate it beyond a repackaged website", and 4.2.2 rules out apps that are mainly "web clippings". A shell needs some genuinely native behaviour — saved cart, native checkout, push-driven flows — to pass review. Plan for that before you treat it as a cheap experiment.

## App or mobile website? Decide this first

Most stores reach for an app to fix a problem an app does not fix.

| Your situation | What you actually need |
|---|---|
| Traffic is low and you want more customers | Marketing and a faster mobile site, not an app |
| Mobile checkout is losing customers | Fix the checkout; an app inherits the same flow |
| Customers buy once and never return | Retention work first; an app nobody reopens is a dead cost |
| You have buyers who reorder every month | **An app makes sense** |
| You want push notifications to drive repeat orders | **An app makes sense** |
| You want an App Store listing for credibility | A real reason, but the weakest one here |

An app is a retention tool, not an acquisition tool. Nobody discovers your store by browsing the App Store; people install it because they already buy from you and want the next order to be faster.

## What moves the price inside the tier

### Catalogue size and product configuration

A hundred products with three variants each is a different build from twenty thousand products with size, colour and warehouse-level stock. The cost is not in storing products; it is in search, filtering, and keeping stock accurate across the app, the website and the warehouse at the same time. Configurable products, where the price depends on a combination of options, push a project toward the top of the $20,000–$65,000 range.

### Payment methods

Card and wallet payments are the in-app payments feature, $3,000–$7,000 (SAR 11,500–26,500 / AED 11,000–25,500 / QAR 11,000–25,500). Every additional local gateway after the first — a second acquirer, a buy-now-pay-later provider — is $1,500–$5,000 (SAR 5,600–19,000 / AED 5,500–18,500 / QAR 5,500–18,000) each, because each one brings its own sandbox, failure handling, refunds and reconciliation. [Payment Gateways in the Gulf](/en/blog/gulf-payment-gateways) covers which ones matter in which country.

### Integration with the store you already run

This is the line most quotes underestimate. Your app has to read the same products, stock and orders as your web store. Salla, for example, publishes [Merchant APIs](https://docs.salla.dev/) for products, orders and stock quantities, so connecting to a hosted platform is predictable work inside the tier. A custom or older system with no API is different: someone has to build that API first, and it can cost as much as the app.

Ask every vendor: *what happens when stock changes on the website while a customer has that item in the app cart?* The answer tells you whether they have planned for synchronisation or will discover it during the build.

### Push notifications that earn their keep

Push notifications are a rate-card feature at $1,500–$3,000 (SAR 5,600–11,500 / AED 5,500–11,000 / QAR 5,500–11,000). That covers sending them. What makes them pay is the logic behind them: abandoned-cart reminders, back-in-stock alerts and order updates triggered by customer behaviour. Blasting "20% off everything" to every user teaches people to switch notifications off. Make sure the quote names the triggered messages, not just "push".

### Social login

Sign in with Apple or Google removes a form from checkout. It is $1,000–$2,000 (SAR 3,800–7,500 / AED 3,700–7,300 / QAR 3,600–7,300) and usually worth it for a shopping app.

## What it costs to keep running

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Maintenance and OS updates | 15 – 20% of the build cost per year |
| [Apple Developer Program](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/) | $99 per year |
| [Google Play registration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en) | $25 once |
| Hosting, push delivery, payment processing | Priced by each provider; get the figure from their pricing page before you sign |
| New features | Scoped and priced like any other rate-card feature |

Maintenance is not padding. iOS and Android each ship a major release every year, and an app that is not updated eventually breaks on new devices. For a $35,000 app, maintenance alone is $5,250–$7,000 a year. [In-House Maintenance or a Contract?](/en/blog/maintenance-inhouse-vs-contract) explains how to structure it.

## Does the maths work for your store?

Run this before you commission anything. The inputs below are an example; replace them with your own.

**Step 1 — What the app must earn back.** A $35,000 build plus one year of maintenance at 15–20% is $40,250–$42,000 over two years — about SAR 151,000–157,500.

**Step 2 — What one extra order is worth.** Say your average order is SAR 200 at a 30% margin: SAR 60 of profit per order.

**Step 3 — How many extra orders the app must create.** SAR 157,500 ÷ SAR 60 ≈ **2,600 additional orders** over two years, or roughly 110 a month.

**Step 4 — Compare that with what you already have.** If 2,000 repeat customers each order four times a year, that is 16,000 orders over two years; the app needs to add about 16% on top. If you have 150 repeat customers ordering four times a year, that is 1,200 orders, and the app would have to more than triple them.

The first store has a realistic case. The second should spend the money on its mobile site and on retention, and revisit the app when the numbers change.

## Cheaper paths worth pricing first

**A progressive web app.** Installable from the browser, works offline, and can send push notifications. On iPhone, web push arrived with iOS 16.4 and only works after the shopper [adds the web app to the Home Screen](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/). Built properly it is simple-tier work, $5,000–$15,000, with no store review. The trade-off is weaker discoverability on iOS. See [Mobile App or Responsive Website?](/en/blog/app-vs-pwa).

**Your platform's own app option.** Hosted platforms sell branded apps on subscription. You get far less control and a fee that never ends, but you are live in days. For a store still proving demand, compare that subscription with the tiers above before you build.

**Fix the mobile site first.** If your mobile checkout takes six steps and loads slowly, an app reproduces the same funnel somewhere new. Fixing it is cheaper and helps every visitor, not only those who install something.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will Apple accept an app that just wraps my store's website?

Usually not on its own. App Review Guideline 4.2 asks for features that go "beyond a repackaged website", and 4.2.2 rejects apps that are mainly web clippings. A shell around your store needs native pieces such as a saved cart, native checkout or push-driven flows. Budget it in the simple tier, $5,000–$15,000, rather than as a quick copy of the site.

### Which tier does a typical Gulf shopping app fall into?

A single-brand store app with catalogue, cart, card and wallet payments, accounts and order tracking is the e-commerce tier: $20,000–$65,000 (SAR 75,000–244,000 / AED 73,500–238,500 / QAR 73,000–236,500). It moves to the advanced tier, $45,000–$100,000, once other merchants sell through it.

### Does adding a buy-now-pay-later provider change the price?

Yes. Each local gateway beyond the first is priced at $1,500–$5,000 because it has its own sandbox, approval, refund and reconciliation work. Two extra providers add $3,000–$10,000.

### Can the app share products and stock with my Salla or Zid store?

Yes, through the platform's APIs; Salla documents Merchant APIs for products, orders and stock quantities. Confirm before signing that two-way stock synchronisation is written into the scope, because it is the piece most often left out.

### What will the app cost in its second year?

Maintenance at 15–20% of the build cost, plus Apple's $99 annual fee, plus hosting and any new features. For a $35,000 app, maintenance alone is $5,250–$7,000.

### How many repeat buyers make an app worth it?

Work it out from the extra orders the app must create. In the example above, a $35,000 app needs about 2,600 additional orders over two years at SAR 60 profit each; that is a 16% lift for a store with 2,000 repeat customers, and an unrealistic one for a store with 150.
