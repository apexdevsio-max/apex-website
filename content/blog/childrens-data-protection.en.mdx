# Children's Data in Apps: Guardian Consent and What It Requires

**If minors use your app, you need consent from their legal guardian, and you need a way to check that the person consenting really is the guardian.** In Saudi Arabia that rule is written down: the PDPL Implementing Regulations require consent from a person with full legal capacity, and for anyone without it the guardian consents only after you take "appropriate steps to verify" the guardianship. Apple and Google add their own rules for apps aimed at children, and those apply in every market.

> Written from an engineering perspective, not legal advice. Current as of November 2026.

This guide covers what those rules mean for the product: who counts as a minor, how to verify a guardian, what to stop collecting, and what the app stores check. ## What the Saudi rules actually say

The [Implementing Regulations of the PDPL](https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/portal/pdp/knowledgecenter/details/PDPL2) do not use the word "child". They work through legal capacity:

- **Article 11(1)(c):** consent must come from a person with full legal capacity.
- **Article 11(1)(d):** consent must be documented in a way that can be verified later.
- **Article 13(1):** the legal guardian acts in the data subject's interest, including exercising their rights and consenting to processing.
- **Article 13(2):** before relying on a guardian's consent, you must take appropriate steps to verify the guardianship.
- **Article 13(3):** the guardian's consent must not harm the data subject's interests, and you must let the data subject exercise their own rights once they reach full capacity.
- **Article 4(7):** if you know a data subject lacks full capacity, the information you give must be in suitable language.

Two things follow. First, **the regulations set no numeric age.** The cut-off is legal capacity, so the age you treat as "minor" is a question for your lawyer, not a number to copy from another market. Second, **a child's data is not automatically "sensitive data"** under the [PDPL](https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/portal/pdp/knowledgecenter/details/PDPL). The law's sensitive categories are things like health, genetic and biometric data. An education app that also records health or biometric data carries both sets of obligations.

**Other Gulf markets.** We could not load the official text of the UAE federal data protection law, so this article does not state its rules on minors. **Ask your advisor:** at what age does the UAE federal law, or the DIFC or ADGM regime if it governs you, require a guardian's consent, and what verification does it expect?

## Does this apply to you?

It is not only "is this a kids' app". The store rules turn on what you actually do:

- Apple's guideline 5.1.4 covers apps in the Kids Category **and** apps that collect, transmit or can share personal information "from a minor".
- Google Play's Families policy covers apps whose declared target audience includes children, with extra rules for "mixed audience" apps that may reach children or users of unknown age.

**Clearly in scope:** education platforms, children's games, family products, tutoring, school management systems, and anything sold to parents for their children.

**Often in scope without intending to be:** social features, user-generated content, and consumer apps with no meaningful age barrier. If your analytics show under-18 users, plan as if the rules apply.

## Verifying the guardian, not just the age

Saudi Article 13(2) asks for "appropriate steps" to verify guardianship, without listing methods. The table below is an engineering view of the options, not a list the regulator has approved.

| Method | Strength | Friction |
|---|---|---|
| Government digital identity of the parent | Strong where available | Low |
| Payment instrument check (small charge, refunded) | Moderate: proves an adult card holder, not guardianship | Moderate |
| Signed consent form with ID document | Strong | High |
| Guardian email confirmation with a follow-up step | Weak to moderate | Low |
| Video or call verification | Strong | Very high |

**What does not verify anything:** an "I am over 18" checkbox, a self-entered birth date, a "my parent agrees" tick, or consent given by the child.

**National digital identity is the practical answer in the Gulf.** A parent who authenticates through a government identity service gives you a strong check with little friction. See [digital identity integration](/en/blog/digital-identity-integration) for what connecting to those services involves.

**Proportion the check to the risk.** A reading app that stores progress needs less than a platform with messaging between users. Whatever you choose, keep the record Article 11(1)(d) asks for: who consented, when, for which version of your notice, and how you verified them.

## Age assurance in practice

Before you can ask for a guardian, you need to know the user is a minor.

**Self-declaration is weak but standard.** It is the usual baseline, but on its own it verifies nothing, so pair it with the guardian step for anything beyond the most basic features.

**Ask neutrally.** "What is your date of birth?" without showing what each answer unlocks gets more honest answers than "Are you over 18?".

**Do not allow free retries.** If a user enters an under-age date and immediately sees the form again, they will try another date. Persist the first answer.

**Be wary of behavioural age estimation.** Inferring age from usage means profiling children to decide whether you may process children's data. That is circular, and it adds processing you then have to justify.

## Collect less

**Every field is a liability.** A learning app does not need a phone number.

**No behavioural advertising.** Google Play bars interest-based advertising and remarketing to children and users of unknown age. Apple says apps in the Kids Category and apps intended primarily for kids should not include third-party advertising, with narrow exceptions for contextual ads. Decide your monetisation model before you build; see [educational app costs](/en/blog/educational-app-cost).

**Restrict third-party SDKs.** Apple's Kids Category bars sending personally identifiable or device information to third parties, and allows third-party analytics only in limited cases that collect nothing identifying. Google Play bars apps that solely target children from transmitting identifiers such as the Android advertising ID, IMEI or MAC address.

**Think hard about messaging and user-generated content.** Any feature that lets children talk to others brings safety duties well beyond data protection. If you do not need it, leave it out.

**Keep less for less time.** Holding a child's data after they stop using the service is hard to justify.

## App store family requirements

**[Apple's Kids Category (guideline 1.3)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)** bars links out of the app, purchasing opportunities and other distractions unless they sit behind a parental gate. Apps in the category may not send personally identifiable information or device information to third parties. Guideline 5.1.4 adds that any app collecting personal information from a minor must have a privacy policy and comply with children's privacy laws. Apple also notes that **a parental gate is generally not the same as parental consent** under those laws, so you need both.

**[Google Play's Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)** requires you to declare your target audience before publishing, to disclose any personal and sensitive information collected from children (including through SDKs), and to use only Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs for ads shown to children or users of unknown age. Google does not fix an age for "children"; it tells developers to check local law with counsel.

**The parental gate** is a check a young child cannot pass, placed before external links, purchases or social features. A plain "Are you a parent?" button is not one.

For the wider submission picture, see [app store requirements](/en/blog/app-store-requirements).

- [ ] Guardian consent flow with a verification step proportionate to the data
- [ ] Consent records: who consented, when, to which notice version, how verified

- [ ] Guardian view of what you hold about the child
- [ ] Guardian-initiated deletion that actually deletes
- [ ] A way for the user to take over their own rights at full legal capacity
- [ ] Privacy policy section that covers children specifically
- [ ] Correct target-age declaration in both stores

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the Saudi PDPL say at what age a child can consent?

No. The Implementing Regulations require consent from a person with full legal capacity (Article 11) and let the legal guardian consent for someone without it (Article 13), but they set no numeric age. Agree the age you will apply with a lawyer.

### Is a child's personal data "sensitive data" under the Saudi PDPL?

Not as a category. The PDPL's sensitive data covers items such as racial or ethnic origin, religious or political belief, security and criminal data, biometric, genetic and health data. Children's data gets its protection through the guardian rules instead. If your app records a child's health or biometric data, the sensitive-data rules apply to that data as well.

### Is a parental gate enough to count as parental consent?

No. Apple's guideline 5.1.4 says the parental gate in the Kids Category "is generally not the same as" securing parental consent under children's privacy laws. The gate stops a child reaching links and purchases; consent is a separate, recorded step by a verified guardian.

### Which SDKs can a children's app use?

On Google Play, ads to children or users of unknown age must come from Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs, and apps that solely target children must not send identifiers such as the Android advertising ID. On Apple, Kids Category apps should not include third-party analytics or advertising, except in limited cases that collect no identifying data. Audit what each SDK sends before you submit.

### How do I verify that the person consenting is the child's guardian?

The Saudi regulations ask for "appropriate steps" without naming methods. A parent's government digital identity gives the strongest check with low friction where it is available; signed forms and video checks are strong but slow. A refunded card charge proves an adult card holder, not guardianship, so treat it as supporting evidence.

### What changes when the child reaches full legal capacity?

Under Article 13(3)(b) of the Saudi Implementing Regulations, you must let the data subject exercise their own rights once they have full capacity. Build a way to hand the account and its consent choices over from the guardian to the user.
