# How Much Does an E-Commerce Store Cost in Saudi Arabia?

A custom online store in Saudi Arabia costs **$20,000 to $65,000** (SAR 75,000–244,000) and takes **4 to 7 months** to build, as of November 2026. A store on a hosted platform such as Salla or Zid has no build fee in that sense: you pay a monthly subscription, and Zid's published plans run from free to **SAR 299 a month**.

Those two numbers explain why quotes for "an online store" can differ by a factor of ten. They are different products: one you rent, one you own. This guide shows what each costs over three years, the Saudi regulatory work that lands on your budget either way, and the costs that never appear in a quote.

Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. Platform subscriptions are the platforms' own published prices.

---

## Hosted, open-source or custom: what you are buying

**Hosted platforms (Salla, Zid, Shopify).** You rent a ready store and customise its look. You can be selling within days, and the upfront cost is your own setup time. You pay the subscription for as long as you trade and you work within what the platform allows.

**Open-source foundations (WooCommerce, Medusa).** A development team shapes an open-source store around your operation. No platform subscription, wide flexibility, and you own the code. It sits at the lower end of the custom range and needs ongoing maintenance.

**Fully custom store.** Built around your sales model: negotiated or tiered wholesale pricing, complex subscriptions, deep integration with an ERP you already run. It sits toward the top of the range.

Both custom options are priced on the e-commerce tier of our rate card. If you want help deciding between them, our [web development service](/en/services/web-development) scopes this before quoting.

## The three-year cost model

Comparing launch prices hides most of the money. Here is the same store priced over three years, using Zid's top published plan for the hosted side and the bottom of our e-commerce tier for the custom side.

| Line item | Hosted (Zid, SAR 299 plan) | Custom store, bottom of the tier |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Your setup time | $20,000 |
| Platform subscription, 36 months | SAR 10,764 (≈ $2,870) | — |
| Maintenance, 3 years at 15–20% | Inside the subscription | $9,000 – $12,000 |
| Paid apps and plugins | As listed in the app store | Built in or scoped |
| Payment processing | Your gateway rate | Your gateway rate |
| **Three-year total before processing fees** | **≈ $2,870 (SAR 10,764)** | **$29,000 – $32,000 (SAR 109,000 – 120,000)** |

**What the model says.** On cost alone, the hosted store wins by a wide margin at almost any size. Do not let anyone sell you a custom build as a saving.

**Where it can flip.** The gap of roughly $26,000 closes only if the platform takes a share of every sale. If your plan charges a per-sale fee of F%, the custom store becomes cheaper once three years of sales × F exceed that gap. At a hypothetical 1% fee, that is about $871,000 in annual sales. Read your own plan's terms for any per-sale fee and plug in the real figure.

**Why stores go custom anyway.** Because they need something the platform will not do: wholesale price lists per customer, an ERP that must own stock and pricing, unusual checkout rules, or full control of the customer database and page speed. Those are business reasons, and they are the right ones.

## Saudi regulatory work that lands on your budget

These are not optional, and skipping them moves the cost to after launch.

**E-invoicing (FATOORAH).** ZATCA requires invoices to be issued through a compliant electronic solution. Phase 1 (generation) has applied since 4 December 2021; Phase 2 (integration), in force in waves since 1 January 2023, requires the solution to be [integrated with ZATCA's systems](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/Introduction/Pages/What-is-e-invoicing.aspx). On a hosted platform, ask whether its invoicing covers your wave. On a custom store, the integration is part of the build: make sure it is a named line in the scope. [ZATCA E-Invoicing](/en/blog/zatca-einvoicing-guide) covers the technical detail.

**VAT registration.** ZATCA's SME guidelines set [mandatory VAT registration](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/%28SMEs%29%20Guidelines%20%E2%80%93%20Zatca%201.pdf) once revenue exceeds SAR 375,000 within twelve months, and allow voluntary registration above SAR 187,500. Your store's prices, invoices and reports have to handle VAT correctly from the day you cross the line.

**Local payment methods.** Saudi shoppers expect mada cards, Apple Pay and usually a buy-now-pay-later option. On a custom store, the first payment integration is inside the e-commerce tier and every additional local gateway is $1,500–$5,000. [Payment Gateways in the Gulf](/en/blog/gulf-payment-gateways) lists which providers matter.

**Personal data.** Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law sets [obligations for controllers and processors](https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/portal/pdp/home) of personal data, which includes every store that holds customer accounts. Privacy notice, consent and data handling belong in the scope of a custom build. Our [PDPL compliance guide](/en/blog/pdpl-compliance-guide) sets out what that means in practice.

## Costs no quote mentions

A build quote covers the store. These items surprise owners after launch:

- **Product photography.** Usually the largest overlooked item for a catalogue of any size.
- **Product descriptions.** Written per product, in Arabic and English if you sell bilingually, and a direct factor in search rankings.
- **Data entry.** Uploading a full catalogue by hand takes weeks of someone's time.
- **Shipping integrations.** Each carrier has its own API for rates and tracking; on a custom store each one is a scoped item.
- **Translation.** Machine-translated product pages undermine trust with Arabic-speaking buyers.
- **Marketing after launch.** A store with no traffic does not sell, however good the build.

Ask every vendor directly: "What does this quote **not** include?" The answer is more useful than any line in the proposal.

## How to spend less without losing out

**Launch with a focused catalogue.** Start with your best-selling lines rather than everything. You test demand, save on photography and data entry, and fund the rest from revenue.

**Start hosted if you are testing the market.** The three-year model above shows why this is the commercially correct choice, not a compromise.

**Do not pay for future features.** Loyalty programmes, affiliate systems and companion apps can be added later, once you know you need them.

**Own your code and your data.** A custom build should come with an explicit clause that the code and the customer database belong to you. On a hosted platform, confirm you can export customers and orders.

**Add a mobile app only after the store proves itself.** [E-Commerce App Cost](/en/blog/ecommerce-app-cost) shows how to test whether your repeat-buyer numbers justify one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is a custom online store in Saudi riyals?

SAR 75,000 to 244,000 ($20,000–$65,000) as of November 2026, built in 4 to 7 months. Each extra local payment gateway adds SAR 5,600–19,000, and maintenance runs 15–20% of the build cost every year.

### Which signs show my store has outgrown a hosted platform?

Cost is rarely the trigger, as the three-year model shows. The real signs are operational: you keep building workarounds for pricing or checkout rules the platform cannot express, your ERP has to own stock and prices, or a per-sale fee in your plan has grown larger than the cost of owning the store.

### Does a custom Saudi store have to connect to ZATCA?

Yes, when it issues invoices. Since Phase 2 began rolling out on 1 January 2023, invoicing solutions in the relevant waves must be integrated with ZATCA's systems, so the integration has to be in the build scope, not added after launch.

### At what revenue does my store have to register for VAT?

Under ZATCA's SME guidelines, registration is mandatory once revenue exceeds SAR 375,000 within twelve months, and voluntary between SAR 187,500 and SAR 375,000.

### How long does it take to launch each option?

A hosted store can be live in 1 to 3 weeks. A custom store takes 4 to 7 months. In both cases, product photos and descriptions are the most common cause of delay, not development.

### How much should I set aside each year for a custom store?

15–20% of the build cost for maintenance and security updates: $3,000–$4,000 a year on a $20,000 store. Add hosting, payment processing and marketing on top.
