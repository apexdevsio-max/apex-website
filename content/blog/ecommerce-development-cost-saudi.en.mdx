# How Much Does an E-Commerce Store Cost in Saudi Arabia? (2026 Guide)

You have decided to sell online, and you started asking for quotes — only to find offers starting at 3,000 SAR and others exceeding 300,000 SAR for what looks like the same project. That spread is not one side exaggerating. It reflects a fundamental difference in what you are actually buying.

**The short answer: an e-commerce store in Saudi Arabia costs between $3,000 (≈ 11,250 SAR) for a hosted platform and $80,000 (≈ 300,000 SAR) for a fully custom build.** The difference is not design quality — it is whether you own the platform and whether it can grow with you.

This guide breaks down the three options available to you, the costs no quote mentions, and the Saudi regulatory requirements that get added to your budget whether you planned for them or not.

---

## The three options: what you are actually buying

Before the numbers, understand that the market sells you three genuinely different products under one name.

**Hosted platforms (Salla, Zid, Shopify)** — you rent a pre-built store and customise its appearance. You can start selling within days, and upfront cost is very low. But you pay a monthly subscription indefinitely, sometimes a percentage of every sale, and you remain bounded by what the platform permits.

**Semi-custom (WooCommerce or Medusa)** — an open-source foundation that a development team shapes around your needs. Moderate upfront cost, no platform subscription, and far wider flexibility. It requires ongoing technical maintenance.

**Fully custom platform** — built from scratch for your business model. Highest cost and longest timeline, but the only viable option when your sales model is unconventional (complex subscriptions, negotiated pricing, deep integration with an existing ERP).

## The calculation that changes the decision: three-year cost

Comparing $5,000 for a hosted store against $25,000 for a custom one looks settled — until you total what you actually pay by year three.

| Line item | Hosted platform | Custom WooCommerce |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | $5,000 | $25,000 |
| Platform subscription (3 years) | $5,400 | $0 |
| Hosting and maintenance (3 years) | Included | $7,500 |
| Platform commission on sales* | $4,500 | $0 |
| Paid apps and extensions | $2,700 | $1,500 |
| **Three-year total** | **$17,600** | **$34,000** |

*\* Assuming $50,000 annual sales and a 3% platform commission. Rates vary by plan and platform.*

**The honest conclusion:** the hosted platform genuinely remains cheaper up to a certain sales volume. The point where the equation flips is around **$150,000 in annual sales** — beyond that, platform commission alone exceeds the cost of building a store you own. Anyone telling you otherwise is selling what they want to sell, not what you need.

## Saudi regulatory requirements that add to your budget

These are not optional, and skipping them does not save money — it defers the cost to post-launch, where it is higher.

**E-invoicing (Fatoora)** — the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority requires stores to issue electronic invoices compliant with Phase Two (integration), including QR codes, digital signatures, and direct integration with the authority's platform. Hosted platforms typically include this; a custom store needs an integration costing **$1,500 – $5,000**. See the [official ZATCA website](https://zatca.gov.sa/) for current requirements.

**Local payment gateways** — accepting **Mada** is not a luxury in the Saudi market; it is the most widely used local payment card. Add **Apple Pay**, **STC Pay**, and buy-now-pay-later options like **Tabby** and **Tamara**, which Saudi shoppers now expect. Each gateway requires integration, testing, and approval: **$1,500 – $4,000 per gateway** on a custom store. For the full detail see our [Gulf payment gateways guide](/en/blog/gulf-payment-gateways).

**Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL)** — Saudi Arabia's PDPL imposes obligations on any platform processing Saudi customer data: a clear privacy policy, a consent mechanism, and data storage controls. Baseline compliance adds **$1,000 – $3,000**.

**Commercial registration** — the Saudi Business Center requires e-commerce stores to be documented. Administrative fees are relatively modest, but it is a mandatory step before operating.

## Hidden costs no quote mentions

A quote covers building the store. These are real line items that surprise owners after launch:

- **Product photography** — $15 – $50 per product done professionally. A 200-product store means $3,000 – $10,000. This is often the single largest overlooked budget item.
- **Product description writing** — $5 – $20 per description, and a direct factor in your search rankings.
- **Data entry** — uploading 500 products manually takes weeks.
- **Shipping carrier integration** — SMSA, Aramex, Naqel: $800 – $2,500 per integration.
- **Professional translation** — if you want a bilingual store, machine translation undermines your credibility.
- **Post-launch marketing** — a store with no traffic does not sell. Budget a monthly marketing spend of at least the store's build cost spread across the first year.

> **Practical rule:** add 30% to any quote you receive as a reserve for the items above. Those who never need it are pleased; those who do are prepared.

## How to reduce cost without losing out

**Start with a limited product count.** Launch with your best 30–50 products rather than the full catalogue. You test the market genuinely, save thousands on photography and data entry, and fund the rest from real revenue.

**Choose a hosted platform if you are starting out.** If your projected sales are under $100,000 annually, a hosted platform is a commercially correct decision — not a compromise. Move to custom when your numbers demand it.

**Do not pay for future features.** Loyalty programmes, affiliate systems, companion apps — all can be added later at lower cost, once you know whether you actually need them.

**Confirm you own your code and your data.** An explicit contract clause stating the code and customer database belong entirely to you. Without it, you are tied to your vendor indefinitely.

**Consider a mobile app only after the store proves itself.** Store first; app once the numbers prove you need one. See our [Gulf app development cost guide](/en/blog/mobile-app-development-cost-gulf) when you reach that stage.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a simple e-commerce store cost in Saudi Arabia?

A basic store on a hosted platform like Salla or Zid starts at $3,000 (≈ 11,250 SAR) including design, setup, and payment integration, with an annual subscription of $1,200 to $3,600. This is the right choice for a first store with fewer than 500 products.

### Is Salla better than Shopify for the Saudi market?

Salla and Zid are built specifically for Saudi Arabia: full Arabic support, ready integration with Mada, Tabby, and Tamara, and built-in Fatoora compliance. Shopify is stronger globally with a wider app ecosystem, but needs middleware for local compliance. For stores selling primarily inside Saudi Arabia, local platforms mean less friction.

### How long does it take to build an e-commerce store?

Hosted platform: 1–3 weeks. Hosted with design customisation: 4–8 weeks. Custom WooCommerce: 8–14 weeks. Built from scratch: 4–8 months. Delays in preparing product photography and content are the most common cause of overrun — not development.

### Do I need a commercial registration to open an online store?

Yes. Commercial activity in Saudi Arabia requires a commercial registration and store documentation through the Saudi Business Center. You must also register for VAT once you exceed the statutory revenue threshold.

### What is the annual cost of running an online store?

Hosted platform: $1,200 – $3,600 per year including hosting and support. Custom store: $2,000 – $6,000 per year for hosting, maintenance, and security updates. Add payment gateway fees to both (2.5–3% per transaction) plus your marketing budget.

### When should I move from a hosted platform to a custom store?

The clearest indicator is the number: once annual sales pass roughly $150,000, platform commission alone exceeds the cost of building a store you own. The second indicator is finding yourself working around platform limitations to execute a basic sales operation in your business model.
