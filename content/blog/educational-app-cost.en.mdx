# Educational App Development Cost: From MVP to Full Platform (2026)

**A course app that plays recorded lessons with simple quizzes costs $5,000–$15,000 (SAR 19,000–56,500 / AED 18,500–55,000 / QAR 18,000–54,500). An interactive app with graded quizzes, progress tracking, subscriptions and a parent or teacher dashboard costs $15,000–$45,000 (SAR 56,500–169,000 / AED 55,000–165,500 / QAR 54,500–164,000), and a school platform or live classes run $45,000–$100,000 (SAR 169,000–375,000 / AED 165,500–367,500 / QAR 164,000–364,000).**

Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. "Educational app" covers very different products, from a recorded-course player to a school system that manages attendance, grades and parents, so the first job is to work out which one you are building. ## Running costs: video is the deciding item

An educational app differs from most others in that **its running cost rises with its success**. Every new learner who watches a video adds streaming and storage charges, billed by usage by your video provider.

- **Video hosting and streaming** — use a specialised streaming service rather than building your own; it is cheaper up front, the quality is better and it scales on its own.
- **Servers and database** — grow with active learners.
- **Maintenance** — 15–20% of the build cost every year.

## Store commission on subscriptions

If you sell subscriptions inside the app, the app stores take a share, and it changes your pricing more than most founders expect:

- **Apple** keeps 30% of an auto-renewable subscription during a subscriber's first year and 15% after one year of paid service. Developers in the [App Store Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) (up to 1 million USD in proceeds a year) pay 15% from the start. Details are on Apple's [subscriptions page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/).
- **Google Play** charges 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions in markets outside Australia, the EEA, Japan, the UK and the US, which includes the Gulf, according to its [service fees page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en).

Build this into your financial model from day one. If your plan depends on small, frequent subscriptions, the commission can change whether the business works at all.

## Protecting minors' data: the item you cannot defer

If your app serves school-age learners, you are handling children's data, and the law treats consent differently.

**In Saudi Arabia**, Article 5 of the [Personal Data Protection Law](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/08/PersonalDataProtectionLaw.pdf) makes consent the default basis for processing and leaves the Implementing Regulations to set how consent is obtained from a legal guardian when the data subject fully or partially lacks legal capacity. Article 20 requires you to notify the regulator of any breach. In practice that means a verifiable guardian consent step, not a checkbox the student can tick.

**Design requirements that follow:**

- A documented, auditable guardian consent mechanism.
- Collecting only the data the learning experience genuinely needs.
- No behavioural advertising aimed at minors.
- Strict controls on any user-to-user messaging.

If you are selling to schools, check the education authority's requirements before building; institutional requirements are often stricter than the general law. Our [children's data protection guide](/en/blog/childrens-data-protection) covers the detail.

## Revenue models and what they add to the build

Your revenue model decides which features you need, so settle it **before** development:

- **Subscription** — needs subscription management, renewals and failed-payment handling. Through the stores, use store billing; on the web, the in-app payments add-on ($3,000–$7,000).
- **One-off course purchase** — the simplest model; in-app payments covers it.
- **Free with paid content** — needs precise entitlement rules for what each learner can open; it usually pushes a simple app into the mid tier.
- **Selling to organisations (B2B)** — needs an admin panel, seat management and reporting; this is advanced-tier territory.

## How do you raise completion rates?

The real problem with educational apps is not downloads but **completion**. These practices act on it most directly:

1. **Short lessons** — a 5–10 minute lesson is far easier to finish than an hour-long lecture.
2. **Visible progress** — a completion bar gives learners a reason to finish.
3. **Smart reminders** — triggered when a learner lapses, not sent daily.
4. **A certificate on completion** — especially effective in professional training.
5. **Offline download** — removes the network barrier altogether.

## Common mistakes that cost the most

**Building the platform before testing the content.** The most expensive mistake in this sector. Release one course on a simple platform, or even to a closed group, and measure completion. If people do not finish your free content, an advanced-tier platform will not save it.

**Underestimating content production.** Many founders put the whole budget into development and forget that **producing the content** — filming, editing and educational review — is a separate and substantial cost. A polished hour of teaching video takes far more work than most project owners expect.

**Ignoring store commission.** In-app subscriptions pay 15–30% to the store, as set out above. Selling on the web avoids it but has its own trade-offs. Model it from the first day.

**Building your own streaming system.** Efficient video hosting and streaming is a hard technical problem that commercial services have already solved at reasonable prices. Building it from scratch drains the budget with no competitive return.

**Ignoring weak networks.** Part of your audience will learn on mobile data or in areas with poor coverage. Offline download is not a luxury; it is a completion factor.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a recorded-course app with quizzes cost?

It is a simple app on our rate card: $5,000–$15,000 (SAR 19,000–56,500 / AED 18,500–55,000 / QAR 18,000–54,500), built in 6–10 weeks. Graded quizzes, progress tracking and subscriptions move it into the mid tier at $15,000–$45,000.

### What separates a course app from a school platform in price?

A course app serves one learner at a time. A school platform manages students, teachers, parents and administrators with attendance, grades and timetables, which makes it an advanced platform at $45,000–$100,000 (SAR 169,000–375,000 / AED 165,500–367,500 / QAR 164,000–364,000), with stricter data protection work on top.

### How much do Apple and Google take from education subscriptions?

Apple takes 30% in a subscriber's first year and 15% afterwards, or 15% throughout for Small Business Program members. Google Play takes 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions in Gulf markets.

### What does Saudi PDPL require for students under 18?

Consent is the default basis for processing, and where the learner lacks full legal capacity it must come from the legal guardian under the conditions in the Implementing Regulations. Collect the minimum data, avoid behavioural advertising and report breaches to the regulator.

### What does adding an AI tutor cost?

The AI/ML integration add-on is $10,000–$40,000+ (SAR 37,500–150,000+ / AED 36,500–147,000+ / QAR 36,500–145,500+) on top of your tier. If the AI tutor is the product itself, it is an AI-powered app at $40,000–$150,000+.

### Should we launch on the web before building an app?

Often yes: a web platform avoids store commission and is quicker to change. Price it with our [website design cost guide](/en/blog/website-design-cost), and move to an app when you need offline download, push reminders, or your numbers show learners prefer mobile.
