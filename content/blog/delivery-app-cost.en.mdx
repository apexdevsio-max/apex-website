# Delivery App Development Cost: The Full Breakdown (2026)

**A delivery app for one business — a customer app, a driver app and an admin panel for a single merchant — is a mid-complexity build at $15,000–$45,000 (SAR 56,500–169,000 / AED 55,000–165,500 / QAR 54,500–164,000), plus $4,000–$8,000 (SAR 15,000–30,000 / AED 14,500–29,500 / QAR 14,500–29,000) for real-time GPS tracking. A multi-vendor marketplace that serves many merchants is an advanced platform at $45,000–$100,000 (SAR 169,000–375,000 / AED 165,500–367,500 / QAR 164,000–364,000).**

Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. The question that decides your number is not how many features you want but how many parties the system serves. The sections below show where each model sits, the running costs that start on launch day, and the cheaper routes worth trying first. ## Three apps, one backend

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Customer app** | Browse, order, pay, track the driver |
| **Driver app** | Accept jobs, navigate, update status, collect cash on delivery |
| **Admin dashboard** | Assign orders, watch the fleet, handle refunds, run reports |
| **Backend** | Order logic, real-time sync, database, notifications |

A delivery app cannot be priced like a brochure app because it is several apps sharing one live backend. **The hardest part is none of the screens; it is keeping all three in sync in real time.** The driver's position updates every few seconds, and a change of status has to reach the customer and the dispatcher at the same moment.

## The cost everyone overlooks: running it

**Building is paid once; operating is paid every month.** None of the items below is a fixed price, because each is billed by usage by the provider you choose:

- **Maps and routing.** [Google Maps Platform](https://mapsplatform.google.com/pricing/) bills pay-as-you-go per API call or map load, with a free monthly allowance per product and volume discounts above that. Every tracking screen and every address search draws on it, so the bill grows with your order count.
- **SMS and one-time passwords.** Billed per message by your SMS provider.
- **Hosting.** Real-time sync needs servers that cope with evening and weekend peaks; see our [hosting comparison](/en/blog/hosting-comparison).
- **Payment processing.** A fee per transaction set by your gateway contract.
- **Store accounts.** Two apps usually means two listings: [Apple charges 99 USD a year](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/) for its developer programme and [Google Play a one-time 25 USD](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en) registration fee.
- **Maintenance.** 15–20% of the build cost every year.

> **Practical rule:** fund a full year of operations before launch. A delivery service that runs out of money after three months loses drivers and customers together, and winning them back is harder than winning them the first time.

## Gulf market requirements

**Payment methods.** Plan the first release around the methods your customers actually use. In Saudi Arabia that means mada cards and wallets such as STC Pay alongside Apple Pay; the UAE and Qatar have their own local rails. Cash on delivery is still widely expected, which costs nothing to build but needs a settlement process with drivers. Our guide to [Gulf payment gateways](/en/blog/gulf-payment-gateways) covers the options by country.

**Arabic and RTL.** Mirrored layouts, maps and notifications in Arabic are part of the build when designed in from the start; retrofitting them later is where the extra cost comes from.

**Location data and Saudi PDPL.** Driver and customer locations are personal data under Saudi Arabia's [Personal Data Protection Law](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/08/PersonalDataProtectionLaw.pdf). Location is not on the law's list of sensitive data, but processing it still needs a lawful basis such as consent (Article 5), and Article 20 requires you to notify the regulator of any breach. Collect location only while a job is active, and say so in the permission prompt.

**Delivery licensing.** Commercial delivery activity can need transport or delivery licences that vary by country, activity and vehicle type. Confirm your position with the regulator in each market before launch, not after.

## Cheaper routes — read this before deciding

- **An off-the-shelf delivery platform** on a monthly subscription launches in weeks and suits testing the market. You do not own the platform and you are limited to what it allows.
- **Listing on an existing delivery aggregator** costs commission on every order but nothing in development. It makes sense until your annual commission approaches the cost of building and maintaining your own app.
- **A driver app only, with orders taken by phone or WhatsApp,** is a simple app at $5,000–$15,000 (SAR 19,000–56,500 / AED 18,500–55,000 / QAR 18,000–54,500) plus the GPS add-on. It suits a small, local operation.
- **An ordering website** with no app is priced as a mid-size company website, $8,000–$25,000 (SAR 30,000–94,000 / AED 29,500–92,000 / QAR 29,000–91,000). Enough if customers order once or twice a month.

**When does a custom build earn its cost?** When your annual aggregator commission exceeds the build cost plus 15–20% yearly maintenance, or when your delivery model is unusual enough that no ready-made platform supports it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a delivery app for one restaurant a mid or an advanced build?

Mid. One merchant with its own drivers is a mid-complexity app at $15,000–$45,000 (SAR 56,500–169,000 / AED 55,000–165,500 / QAR 54,500–164,000), plus $4,000–$8,000 for live GPS tracking. It becomes advanced when you add other merchants.

### What does live driver tracking add to the quote?

The rate-card price for real-time GPS tracking is $4,000–$8,000 (SAR 15,000–30,000 / AED 14,500–29,500 / QAR 14,500–29,000). The map usage it generates is a separate monthly cost billed by your maps provider.

### Will Google Maps charge me for every driver on the road?

Google Maps Platform bills per API call or map load rather than per driver, with a free monthly allowance per product. More drivers and more tracking screens mean more calls, so set usage quotas and alerts from the first day.

### Is driver location sensitive data under Saudi PDPL?

It is personal data, but it is not on the law's list of sensitive categories, which covers items such as health, genetic and biometric data. You still need a lawful basis such as consent and must report breaches, so collect location only during active deliveries.

### When does building a marketplace beat listing on an aggregator?

When the commission you pay each year exceeds the cost of an advanced platform at $45,000–$100,000 plus 15–20% annual maintenance, and you can recruit drivers and merchants yourself. Until then, listing is cheaper.

### Which payment methods should the first release support?

The ones your market uses most: in Saudi Arabia, mada and Apple Pay, plus cash on delivery with a driver settlement process. Each additional local gateway after the first costs $1,500–$5,000 (SAR 5,600–19,000 / AED 5,500–18,500 / QAR 5,500–18,000).
