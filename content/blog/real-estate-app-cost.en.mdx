# How Much Does a Real Estate App Cost?

**A brokerage or developer app that publishes its own listings, with search, filters and map search, is a mid-complexity build at $15,000–$45,000 (SAR 56,500–169,000 / AED 55,000–165,500 / QAR 54,500–164,000). A portal that accepts listings from agents and owners you do not employ is an advanced platform at $45,000–$100,000 (SAR 169,000–375,000 / AED 165,500–367,500 / QAR 164,000–364,000).**

Ranges from our published rate card, as of November 2026. The tier depends less on the features you list than on one question: who supplies the listings, and who is responsible when one of them is wrong? A brokerage publishing its own inventory is building a catalogue with good search. A portal is building a moderation and trust system that happens to have an app attached. The screens look similar; the projects are not. Our [mobile app development service](/en/services/mobile-apps) scopes property apps against these tiers.

| Portal / marketplace | Agents and owners you do not employ | Advanced app or platform | $45,000 – $100,000 | 169,000 – 375,000 | 165,500 – 367,500 | 164,000 – 364,000 | 5 – 9 months |

The tier changes at the third row because you stop controlling the data. Duplicate listings, stale prices, properties already sold and fake posts all become your problem, and solving them takes moderation tools, verification and staff, not just code.

If you are a brokerage, stay in the first row. "Opening it up to other agents later" is how a mid-tier project becomes an advanced one.

## What actually drives the cost

### Map search is not a map

Dropping pins on a map is a day's work. Property map search is not.

At a few hundred listings you can draw every pin. At twenty thousand you cannot; the app freezes. You need server-side clustering that groups listings by zoom level, returns counts rather than records when zoomed out, and stays responsive while the user pans. Add draw-your-own-area search and you are running geospatial queries that need the database designed for them from the start.

That is why map search is the feature most likely to push a brokerage app to the upper end of the mid tier. Ask any vendor how they handle twenty thousand pins at city zoom; the answer separates those who have built it from those who have not.

### Media is the hidden budget

A listing has twenty photographs. A thousand listings have twenty thousand images, each needing several sizes for thumbnails, cards and full view.

Uploading, resizing, storing and serving those quickly is real infrastructure. Agents will upload large photos straight from their phones, and if you do not process them, the app is slow for everyone. Expect storage and bandwidth to be a running cost that grows with your catalogue, not a fixed one.

### Keeping listings honest

The complaint that kills property apps is not missing features. It is calling about a listing that sold three weeks ago.

Preventing that means listing expiry, required refresh intervals, agent accountability, and a way for users to report a stale post. None of it is glamorous and all of it is necessary; on a portal it is a core part of the advanced tier. The rest is operational discipline rather than software.

### Virtual tours

Embedding a 360° tour viewer is a modest piece of work inside the tier. Producing the tours is a separate, ongoing cost per property. It is worth it for high-value homes and off-plan units the buyer cannot visit, and rarely for a rental that will turn over in a month.

## Regulatory requirements in the Gulf

This is where property apps differ from other marketplaces, and where a generic vendor misses things.

**Saudi Arabia.** Brokerage is licensed by the Real Estate General Authority (REGA). Under the [Implementing Regulations of the Real Estate Brokerage Law](https://rega.gov.sa/en/rules-regulations-and-guidelines/regulations/implementing-regulations-of-real-estate-brokerage-law/), a broker must obtain a real estate advertisement licence for each advertisement (Article 6), and leaving the licensed person's name or licence number out of an advertisement is listed as a violation. Electronic property platforms are licensed too: REGA reported in April 2025 that it had [licensed 71 platforms](https://rega.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/rega-licenses-71-electronic-real-estate-platforms/), each after meeting its conditions and completing technical integration with REGA. If your app handles rentals, plan how contracts reach [Ejar](https://rega.gov.sa/en/media-center/news-announcements/ejar-registers-over-10-million-rental-contracts-since-launch/), REGA's electronic network that documents residential and commercial rental contracts.

**UAE.** Each emirate regulates separately. In Dubai, property advertising needs a [real estate ad permit from the Dubai Land Department](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-a-real-estate-permit), applied for through the Trakheesi system. Most permits require a copy of the marketing contract with the property owner, and off-plan projects not registered with the escrow account department cannot be advertised.

**What this means for the build.** The fields your listing form requires — licence numbers, advertisement licence or permit references, contract references — are set by these rules. Confirm them with a local adviser before you design the form; adding a mandatory field after launch means going back to every existing listing. For data handling, see our [PDPL compliance guide](/en/blog/pdpl-compliance-guide) and the [UAE data protection guide](/en/blog/uae-data-protection-guide).

## The lead problem

A property app exists to generate enquiries, and most apps handle the moment of enquiry badly.

A user who taps "contact agent" at 11pm expects something to happen. If the lead sits in an inbox until Sunday morning, it is dead; by then they have contacted three other listings. Speed of response decides whether the app produces business.

So the lead flow deserves more attention than it usually gets: instant notification to the assigned agent, escalation if nobody answers within a set time, and visibility for management into who responds and who does not. Push notifications are the cheapest way to make that instant; treat the whole flow as the part of the app that decides the return on everything else.

## Running costs

| Item | How it is billed |
|---|---|
| Maintenance | 15–20% of the build cost per year |
| Maps and geocoding | Pay-as-you-go per API call or map load |
| Image storage and bandwidth | By usage, growing with the catalogue |
| Virtual tour hosting | By the tour provider |
| Listing moderation | Staff time |

Maps deserve a warning. [Google Maps Platform](https://mapsplatform.google.com/pricing/) bills per API call or map load, with a free monthly allowance per product, and a property app loads maps constantly. A successful app can generate a maps bill larger than its hosting. Set quotas and alerts from the first day, and compare alternatives before your traffic makes the choice expensive.

## Is it worth building?

For a **brokerage**, compare the app against your portal listing fees and lead costs. If you pay for premium placement and buy leads on top, an app that captures repeat visitors directly reduces both over time. The catch: portals supply discovery your app cannot. The app serves people who already know your brand.

The realistic case for a brokerage app is not replacing the portal. It is owning the relationship with buyers who have already dealt with you, and giving your agents a faster tool than WhatsApp screenshots.

For a **developer selling its own projects**, the case is stronger. Off-plan buyers make a large decision from limited information, and an app with floor plans, payment schedules and construction updates does real work. Here the app is a sales tool for a specific inventory, and it can be retired when the project sells out.

For a **portal**, the app is a fraction of the business. Listing supply, licensing and traffic acquisition are the business.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a brokerage app showing our own listings cost?

It is a mid-complexity app on our rate card: $15,000–$45,000 (SAR 56,500–169,000 / AED 55,000–165,500 / QAR 54,500–164,000), built in 3–5 months, including search, filters, map search and a listing admin.

### Why does a property portal cost a full tier more?

Because you no longer control the listings. Agent accounts, moderation, expiry, verification and lead routing put a portal in the advanced tier at $45,000–$100,000 (SAR 169,000–375,000 / AED 165,500–367,500 / QAR 164,000–364,000), and it needs staff to run.

### Does a property platform need a REGA licence in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. REGA licenses electronic property platforms after they meet its conditions and integrate technically with REGA, and each advertisement needs its own advertising licence under the brokerage law's Implementing Regulations. Build those references into the listing form from the start.

### What does Dubai require before a listing is advertised in the app?

A real estate ad permit from the Dubai Land Department, applied for through Trakheesi, usually with a copy of the marketing contract with the owner. Off-plan projects not registered with the escrow account department cannot be advertised.

### Is in-app chat worth adding for buyers and agents?

If your agents now work over WhatsApp, yes: in-app chat costs $5,000–$10,000 (SAR 19,000–37,500 / AED 18,500–36,500 / QAR 18,000–36,500) and keeps every enquiry inside your lead system, where management can see response times.

### How do we keep map costs under control once traffic grows?

Google Maps Platform bills per API call or map load after a free monthly allowance, so cluster pins on the server, cache geocoding results, and set quotas and billing alerts before launch.
