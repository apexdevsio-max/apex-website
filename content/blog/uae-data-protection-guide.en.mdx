# UAE Data Protection Law: A Developer's Guide

**Which UAE data protection law governs your product depends on where your entity is incorporated.** A mainland company follows Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021, in force since 2 January 2022 and supervised by the UAE Data Office. A company in the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC) follows DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020, and one in Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) follows the ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021. Getting this wrong means complying with the wrong statute.

> Written from an engineering perspective, not legal advice. Current as of November 2026.

This guide is the UAE detail behind the [Gulf regulatory compliance guide](/en/blog/gulf-compliance-guide), which compares the UAE with Saudi Arabia and Qatar. It covers what each UAE regime says, what to build, and the open questions to take to a UAE adviser.

---

## First: which regime governs you?

| Your entity is incorporated in | Regime | Regulator |
|---|---|---|
| Mainland UAE | Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021 | UAE Data Office |
| **DIFC** | DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020 | DIFC Commissioner of Data Protection |
| **ADGM** | ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021 | ADGM Office of Data Protection |
| Health services, anywhere in the UAE | Data protection law **plus** health-sector rules | Health authority (for example DHA in Dubai) |

**Why teams get this wrong:** "we're a Dubai company" is ambiguous. DIFC is a separate jurisdiction with its own statute; the [DIFC Commissioner's FAQ](https://www.difc.com/business/registrars-and-commissioners/commissioner-of-data-protection/faqs-glossary) states that the DIFC law applies to a controller or processor incorporated in DIFC regardless of whether the processing takes place in DIFC, and can also reach non-DIFC entities that process personal data in DIFC on an ongoing basis. Your trade licence tells you which regime you are in.

> **Questions for your lawyer:** if you are in a free zone other than DIFC or ADGM, which regime applies to you? And if your group has a DIFC or ADGM entity and a mainland entity, which entity is the controller for which processing, and does any processing fall under more than one regime?

## The federal law: what we could verify

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws) summarises Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021 as follows:

- It applies to processing of personal data, wholly or partly through electronic systems, **inside or outside the country**.
- It **prohibits processing without the data owner's consent**, with exceptions such as processing necessary to protect a public interest or to carry out legal procedures and rights.
- Individuals can **request correction** of inaccurate data and can **restrict or stop processing** of their data.
- It sets requirements for **cross-border transfer and sharing** of personal data.
- The **UAE Data Office** (Federal Decree-Law 44 of 2021) is the federal regulator, responsible for policies, monitoring standards, complaints and implementing guidelines.

We could not open the official text of the decree-law on the UAE legislation portal while preparing this guide, so we have not relied on article-level detail from secondary summaries. Take these questions to a UAE adviser:

> **Questions for your UAE adviser:**
> 1. Have the Executive Regulations of Decree-Law 45/2021 been issued, and from what date must we comply with them?
> 2. Do they set a deadline in hours or days for notifying the Data Office of a breach?
> 3. What is the full list of cases where we may process without consent, and which fits each of our processing activities?
> 4. What conditions apply to transferring data to our hosting region and to each vendor abroad?
> 5. What administrative fines apply, and who imposes them?
> 6. Is the UAE Data Office still the supervising body, or has supervision moved to another federal authority?

## Consent, and why "consent for everything" is a trap

Because the federal law starts from consent with exceptions, most products need a consent layer. Build it properly:

```
□ Separate opt-in per purpose (service / marketing / analytics)
□ Never pre-ticked
□ Plain-language explanation of each purpose, in Arabic and in English if your users need it
□ Withdrawal as easy as granting it
□ Timestamped record: when, and against which policy version
```

**But do not ask for consent you do not need.** If a processing activity falls under one of the law's exceptions, asking for consent anyway creates a worse position: a user who withdraws it leaves you unable to do something you were entitled, or obliged, to do. Map each processing activity to its basis with your adviser, then build consent only where consent is the basis.

## Rights: build restriction, not just deletion

The portal confirms two rights that most implementations under-build: correction, and the right to **restrict or stop** processing.

| Request | What you build |
|---|---|
| See my data | A "my data" page and export |
| Correct my data | Editable fields and a correction request path |
| Delete my data | Real deletion that reaches backups, logs and exports |
| Restrict processing | A status that every processing path checks before using the record |
| Stop a specific processing | Per-purpose switches (for example, stop profiling but keep the account) |
| Withdraw consent | One-click revocation per purpose |

**Restriction is the one most often missing.** It is not deletion; it is "keep it but stop using it". In most systems that means a flag that your jobs, analytics exports and marketing sync must actually respect. Building deletion without restriction looks complete and is not.

The implementation pattern overlaps heavily with Saudi Arabia's, so the [Saudi PDPL developer guide](/en/blog/pdpl-compliance-guide) is worth reading alongside this one.

## Breach notification by regime

| Regime | Deadline | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ADGM | "Without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after becoming aware" (Article 32) | [ADGM Office of Data Protection](https://www.adgm.com/operating-in-adgm/office-of-data-protection/data-breach-notifications) |
| DIFC | "As soon as practicable in the circumstances" (Article 41); affected individuals where the breach is likely to pose a high risk (Article 42) | [DIFC Commissioner FAQ](https://www.difc.com/business/registrars-and-commissioners/commissioner-of-data-protection/faqs-glossary) |
| Federal | No hour count we could verify as of November 2026 (see the questions above) | — |

**Do not plan around "72 hours" for the federal law as if it were settled,** and do not plan around having more time either. The engineering is the same whatever the number:

- **Detection:** alerting on anomalous access or bulk export.
- **Forensics:** logs detailed enough to establish what was accessed, when, and whose data.
- **A written procedure:** who is told internally, who decides, who contacts the regulator.
- **Vendor terms** requiring your processors to notify **you within 24 hours**, since their delay consumes your window.

If you also serve Saudi users, Saudi Arabia's 72-hour rule applies to that data and sets the pace for the whole procedure.

## Transfers and residency

**Health data is the hard rule.** Dubai Health Authority's [Standards for Telehealth Services](https://dha.gov.ae/uploads/012023/Standards%20for%20Telehealth%20Services2023158613.pdf) (issue 4, effective 26 November 2025) state that storing, developing or transferring health data outside the UAE is prohibited unless DHA approves (section 8.2.5(d)). That is an architectural constraint: hosting, backups, logs and any AI service that sees clinical content must stay in the UAE. The [UAE health apps guide](/en/blog/health-apps-uae-compliance) covers DHA, Abu Dhabi and federal health rules in detail.

**For other data under the federal law,** the law sets transfer requirements but we have not verified their detail from the official text; that is question 4 above.

**DIFC** no longer issues permits for transfers out of DIFC; transfers are assessed against the law's grounds, and DIFC publishes standard contractual clauses adapted from the EU clauses and the UK IDTA ([DIFC FAQ](https://www.difc.com/business/registrars-and-commissioners/commissioner-of-data-protection/faqs-glossary)).

Wherever your data lands, the technical check is identical: where is the primary data, where are the backups, and where are the logs. The [data residency guide](/en/blog/data-residency-gulf) walks through each.

## DIFC and ADGM: the extra paperwork

- **DIFC** requires entities to [notify the Commissioner of their processing operations](https://www.difc.com/business/registrars-and-commissioners/commissioner-of-data-protection/notifications) through the DIFC Client Portal, to notify any change within 14 days of starting the new processing, and to renew the notification with the licence.
- **ADGM** expects a representative of the data controller to file breach notifications to the Office of Data Protection on the controller's behalf.

Build your data map and processing register in a form you can hand straight to these filings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a DIFC company also have to follow UAE Federal Decree-Law 45/2021?

A DIFC-incorporated company follows the DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020, which applies to it regardless of where the processing happens. Whether any federal obligations also reach a particular processing activity is a question for your adviser, especially if your group has a mainland entity too.

### What is the breach notification deadline in ADGM and DIFC?

ADGM requires notice to its Office of Data Protection without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware (Article 32). DIFC requires notice to the Commissioner as soon as practicable in the circumstances (Article 41), and to individuals where the breach is likely to pose a high risk.

### Is there a 72-hour breach deadline under the UAE federal law?

We could not verify one from a primary source as of November 2026. Ask a UAE adviser whether the Executive Regulations have set a deadline. In the meantime, build detection and a written procedure that can meet 72 hours, which is the strictest window in the region.

### Can a mainland UAE app process data without consent?

In defined cases, yes. The UAE government portal describes the federal law as prohibiting processing without consent, with exceptions such as protecting a public interest or carrying out legal procedures and rights. Map each activity to its basis with an adviser rather than asking for consent you do not need.

### What is the difference between deleting and restricting a user's data?

Deleting removes the data. Restricting keeps it but stops you using it, which means a status every processing path must check. The federal law gives individuals the right to restrict or stop processing, so build both.

### Can UAE health app data be hosted outside the UAE?

Not without approval. Dubai Health Authority's telehealth standard prohibits storing, developing or transferring health data outside the UAE unless DHA approves, which covers backups, logs and AI services as well as the main database.
