# Salla or Zid? A Practical Comparison

If you are opening a store in Saudi Arabia and have narrowed the choice to Salla or Zid, the honest starting point is this: **for most stores, either will work.**

Both are Saudi platforms built for this market. Both handle Arabic natively rather than as a translation layer, both integrate Mada and the local buy-now-pay-later providers, and both handle ZATCA e-invoicing without middleware. Neither is going to fail you on the fundamentals.

So this guide is not about which platform is better. It is about the specific differences that make one of them a better fit for **your** store, and the questions worth asking before you commit — because migrating later costs far more than choosing carefully now.

For where hosted platforms sit against custom builds, see [Shopify or a Custom Store?](/en/blog/shopify-vs-custom-store) and our [Saudi e-commerce cost guide](/en/blog/ecommerce-development-cost-saudi).

## What they have in common

It is worth being explicit about this, because most comparison articles manufacture differences that do not exist.

Both platforms give you:

- Native Arabic and RTL, built in rather than retrofitted
- Mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay, Tabby, and Tamara
- ZATCA Phase Two e-invoicing compliance
- Integration with Saudi shipping companies
- Arabic-language support during Saudi business hours
- Themes, a product catalogue, discount codes, and basic reporting
- A mobile app for managing the store

If a comparison tells you one of them has Arabic support and the other does not, or that one handles Mada and the other does not, it is out of date or wrong.

## Where they actually differ

### App and integration ecosystem

Salla has been building its app marketplace longer and has more third-party integrations available — accounting tools, marketing automation, inventory systems, and niche add-ons.

This matters more than it appears. The integrations you need are rarely obvious at launch; they emerge in month four when you realise you need your orders to reach your accounting software automatically. A larger ecosystem means a higher chance the connector already exists rather than needing to be built.

**Check before choosing:** list the systems your business already runs — accounting, warehouse, CRM — and confirm each has an integration on the platform you are considering. This single check predicts more future frustration than any feature comparison.

### Checkout and conversion control

How much you can modify the checkout flow differs, and both platforms restrict it more than a custom store would.

If your business needs something unusual at checkout — a required field for B2B customers, a delivery date picker, conditional shipping rules by product type — verify it is possible before signing up. Hosted platforms are convenient precisely because they constrain you, and checkout is where that constraint is felt most.

### Developer access and APIs

Both expose APIs, and both support custom theme development. The practical questions are how complete the API is for what you need, and how easy it is to find a developer who has worked with it.

If you plan to connect a mobile app to your store later, this becomes a primary consideration rather than a secondary one — see our [e-commerce app cost guide](/en/blog/ecommerce-app-cost) for what that integration involves.

### Pricing structure

Both use tiered subscriptions, and both have commission on some plans. The published numbers change often enough that quoting them here would mislead you within months.

What matters is the **structure** rather than today's figure:

- Does the plan take a percentage of sales, or is it flat?
- At what sales volume does the next tier become cheaper than the commission?
- Which features are gated behind higher tiers — and are any of them ones you need immediately?

Run your projected annual revenue through both platforms' current pricing pages before deciding. A plan that is cheaper at 50,000 SAR monthly may be more expensive at 300,000 SAR.

## Which fits which store

| Your situation | Leans toward |
|---|---|
| First store, learning as you go | Either — pick on interface preference |
| You need many third-party integrations | Salla's larger ecosystem |
| You have a developer and want API depth | Verify both against your specific needs |
| Selling mainly outside Saudi Arabia | Neither — consider Shopify |
| Unusual checkout or pricing rules | Neither — you need custom, see [Shopify or Custom](/en/blog/shopify-vs-custom-store) |
| Under 500 products, standard retail | Either works well |
| Wholesale or B2B with negotiated pricing | Usually neither; both are built for retail |

The honest summary: if your store is conventional Saudi retail, choose on the interface you prefer after trialling both. If your store is unusual, the question is not Salla or Zid — it is whether a hosted platform suits you at all.

## The question that matters more than the choice

**How do you get your data out?**

This is the question almost nobody asks at signup and everybody asks eventually. Before committing, confirm:

- Can you export your full product catalogue, including images and variants?
- Can you export customer records and complete order history?
- Do you keep your domain, or is it registered through the platform?
- What happens to your data if you stop paying?

The answers determine whether you are a customer or a hostage. Both platforms permit export, but the completeness of that export — particularly order history and customer records — is worth verifying in writing before you have three years of business inside one of them.

The same principle applies to any hosted platform, and it is covered more generally in [Template or Custom Design?](/en/blog/template-vs-custom-design).

## How to decide in a week

Rather than reading comparisons indefinitely:

1. **Trial both.** Both offer trial periods. Build the same three products on each and take a test order through to completion.
2. **Test the checkout on a phone.** Most of your customers will buy on mobile. Whichever checkout feels faster to you will feel faster to them.
3. **Contact support with a real question** on both, and note how long the reply takes and whether it answers what you asked.
4. **Check your required integrations** exist on each.
5. **Run your revenue through both pricing pages** at your expected volume, not today's.

Whichever wins on those five, choose it and stop researching. The difference between them is smaller than the cost of another month spent deciding.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Salla better than Zid?

Neither is better in general. Both are Saudi platforms with native Arabic, Mada, and ZATCA compliance. Salla has a larger third-party app ecosystem, which matters if you need many integrations. For a conventional retail store, trial both and choose on interface preference.

### Which is cheaper?

Both use tiered subscriptions with commission on some plans, and published pricing changes often. Run your projected annual revenue through both platforms' current pricing pages — the cheaper option at low volume is not always the cheaper option at high volume.

### Can I move from one to the other later?

You can export and re-import products, but migration is disruptive: URLs change, customer accounts do not transfer cleanly, and order history usually does not follow. Verify export completeness before you commit rather than after.

### Do both support ZATCA e-invoicing?

Yes. Both handle Phase Two e-invoicing without additional middleware, which is one of the main reasons to choose a local platform over an international one for the Saudi market.

### Should I use Shopify instead?

If you sell mainly outside the region, Shopify's international reach and app ecosystem are stronger. For a store selling primarily in Saudi Arabia, the local platforms handle Arabic, Mada, and ZATCA more directly. See [Shopify or a Custom Store?](/en/blog/shopify-vs-custom-store).

### When should I leave hosted platforms entirely?

When the platform's constraints start costing you sales — an unusual checkout you cannot build, pricing rules the platform does not support, or a deep ERP integration. Below roughly 1,000 orders a month, a hosted platform is almost always the better economic choice.

### Can I connect a mobile app to either?

Yes, both expose APIs that a mobile app can read products, stock, and orders from. Confirm the specific endpoints you need exist before building, and budget $3,000 to $8,000 for the integration.

## Conclusion

Salla and Zid are close enough that the comparison is less useful than most articles pretend. Both handle the Saudi essentials — Arabic, Mada, ZATCA, local shipping — without compromise, and neither will hold back a conventional retail store.

Spend your effort on two things instead: check that your required integrations exist on the platform you pick, and confirm in writing how you would export everything if you left. Those two answers will matter to you in three years. The interface you preferred at signup will not.
