# Payment Gateways in the Gulf: Mada, STC Pay, Tabby, and Tamara

**For a store selling in Saudi Arabia, launch with mada, Visa and Mastercard, and Apple Pay through one SAMA-licensed payment service provider, then add Tabby or Tamara once your sales data shows the basket size to justify them.** On our rate card the first payment integration in a custom store costs $3,000–$7,000 (SAR 11,300–26,300) and each additional local gateway $1,500–$5,000 (SAR 5,600–18,800), as of November 2026. On Salla or Zid most of this is a setting rather than a build.

One change matters before you plan: **STC Pay is now STC Bank.** Its e-wallets have been converted into digital bank accounts under a SAMA banking licence, so ask your provider how it accepts STC Bank payments instead of scoping a separate wallet integration.

## The quick map

| Method | What it is | When to add it |
|---|---|---|
| **mada** | The card service of the Saudi Payments Network — the debit cards Saudi banks issue | At launch, for any store selling in Saudi Arabia |
| **Visa / Mastercard** | International card schemes | At launch; usually in the same provider integration as mada |
| **Apple Pay** | Wallet payments on Apple devices | At launch, if your provider supports it with mada |
| **Tabby / Tamara** | Buy now, pay later (BNPL), licensed by SAMA | After launch, once your average basket justifies the higher fee |
| **STC Bank** | Formerly the STC Pay wallet, now a digital bank | Ask your provider; often no separate integration is needed |
| **Cash on delivery** | Cash at handover | Optional; a trust-builder for first-time buyers |

The common mistake is building with a global template that only takes Visa and Mastercard. A Saudi bank's debit card runs on mada, and a checkout that does not show it loses buyers at the final step — after you have already paid to bring them there.

If you are building the store yourself or with us, this is part of the checkout work our [web development team](/en/services/web-development) scopes up front, because changing providers later means re-testing every payment path.

## mada — the method you cannot skip

SAMA describes [mada as the commercial name for the Saudi Payments Network's ATM and point-of-sale card service](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/node/6867), positioned as the first choice for payment in stores. In practice it is the brand on the debit cards Saudi banks issue.

**What this means for your store:** if mada is missing, a buyer with a local debit card cannot pay. That is the most expensive point at which to lose a customer.

**Integration:** Salla and Zid offer it through the platform (on Zid, advanced payment methods start from the paid Rise plan). A custom store connects through a licensed payment service provider, which usually bundles mada, Visa, Mastercard and Apple Pay in one integration — priced on our rate card as the first payment integration, $3,000–$7,000.

## Buy now, pay later (Tabby and Tamara)

**Tabby** and **Tamara** let the buyer split the price into instalments while you receive the amount from the provider, in exchange for a higher merchant fee than cards. Both are SAMA-regulated: SAMA announced it had [licensed Tabby finance to engage in BNPL activity](https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-US/MediaCenter/News/Pages/news-1116.aspx), and BNPL is a licensed activity in the Kingdom.

**When to add them:** when your average order is high enough that paying in parts changes the decision — furniture, electronics, fashion above everyday prices. On low-priced products the extra fee rarely pays for itself. Turn it on, then compare completion rate and order value in your own data before and after.

**Cost:** each is an additional local gateway on our rate card, $1,500–$5,000 (SAR 5,600–18,800) to integrate in a custom store, plus the provider's merchant fee, which is set in your agreement.

## STC Pay is now STC Bank

STC's own notice states that [STC Bank "has now transformed from an E-wallet account to a digital bank account"](https://stcbank.com.sa/en/notice-of-transformation-to-stc-bank) and is licensed by SAMA for digital banking. For a merchant this means two things:

- **Don't scope an "STC Pay integration"** from an old requirements list. Ask your payment provider which STC Bank payment options it supports and whether they come through your existing integration.
- **Update your checkout wording.** Labels and icons that still say "STC Pay" confuse buyers who now use the STC Bank app.

## Cash on delivery — a trust tool, not a default

Many first-time buyers from an unfamiliar store still prefer to pay at the door. The costs are real: refused deliveries, cash settlement with the courier, and slower cash flow.

**The recommendation:** offer it at launch if your category needs it, confirm each order by message before shipping, and move buyers to prepayment with a small incentive (a discount or free shipping).

## The recommended order

**Phase one (launch):**
1. **mada, Visa and Mastercard** through one SAMA-licensed provider.
2. **Apple Pay** through the same provider, if it supports it.
3. **Cash on delivery** only if your category needs it to win first-time buyers.

**Phase two (after proving sales):**
4. **Tabby or Tamara** — if your average order is high.
5. **STC Bank payments** — if your provider does not already cover them.

**Why this order?** Every integration costs money, time and testing. Start with what lets every local buyer pay, and add the rest when your data shows the need.

## The regulatory side

**You need a licensed provider, not a licence of your own.** Saudi Arabia's Law of Payments and Payment Services states, in [Article 4](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/node/1195), that "a person may not operate a payment system or provide payment services in the Kingdom unless licensed by SAMA." Check your provider on SAMA's list of licensed entities before signing.

**Also verify:**
- **E-invoicing (FATOORAH)** — ZATCA's [electronic invoicing requirements](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/Introduction/Pages/What-is-e-invoicing.aspx) apply to your sales invoices.
- **Personal data** — payment and customer data fall under the Personal Data Protection Law; see our [PDPL compliance guide](/en/blog/pdpl-compliance-guide).
- **Card data security** — [PCI DSS](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/standards/pci-dss/) applies to anyone who stores, processes or transmits cardholder data. Use the provider's hosted fields or redirect so card numbers never touch your servers.

> **A security rule:** if anyone suggests storing card numbers in your own database, end the conversation there.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Saudi online store launch without mada?

Technically yes, commercially no. mada is the card service of the Saudi Payments Network, and a buyer paying with a local debit card cannot complete checkout without it. Treat it as mandatory for any store selling inside Saudi Arabia.

### How much does it cost to add mada, Apple Pay and Tabby to a custom store?

On our rate card, the first provider integration covering mada, cards and Apple Pay costs $3,000–$7,000 (SAR 11,300–26,300), and Tabby adds $1,500–$5,000 (SAR 5,600–18,800), as of November 2026. Transaction fees come on top and are set by each provider.

### Is buy-now-pay-later worth its higher merchant fee?

For high-priced items, often; for everyday low-priced products, rarely. The only reliable answer is your own data: switch it on, then compare checkout completion and average order value against the period before.

### What is the difference between a payment method and a payment service provider?

The method is what the buyer sees — mada, Apple Pay, Tabby. The payment service provider is the SAMA-licensed company that connects you to those methods, processes the transactions and settles the money to your account. One provider usually gives you several methods through a single integration.

### Do I still need to integrate STC Pay separately?

Usually not. STC Pay's wallets have been converted into STC Bank digital bank accounts, so ask your payment provider which STC Bank options it supports and whether they arrive through the integration you already have.

### How do I cut refusals on cash-on-delivery orders?

Confirm each order by message or call before shipping, offer a small incentive for paying in advance, and limit cash on delivery to repeat customers once you have enough of them.
